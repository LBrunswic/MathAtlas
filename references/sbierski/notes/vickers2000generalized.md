# vickers2000generalized — Generalized hyperbolicity in conical spacetimes

J. A. Vickers, J. P. Wilson
Classical and Quantum Gravity 17 (2000) 1333–1360 · 2000 · arXiv:gr-qc/9907105 / doi:10.1088/0264-9381/17/6/302 · `pdf/vickers2000generalized.pdf` (arXiv v1, 30 Jul 1999)

**Tier: T2** — peer-reviewed (CQG), complete energy-estimate proof of the Colombeau existence/uniqueness theorem; built on by Hörmann (hormann2015conical) and cited in the Steinbauer–Vickers review, but never independently re-proved (Hörmann says later theory still does not cover it). The distributional-limit part (§5, App. C) is more heuristic than §4.
**Relevance: 3** — Q3 (the reference well-posedness result on a conical spacetime, and the explicit statement of why the cone lies outside Geroch–Traschen), Q2/Q4 (its domain of dependence is the causal past through the axis, which is ETT's J⁻ on E_α).
**Read:** full text (§1–§6 and Appendices A–C; Appendix B skimmed: standard Friedlander/Bruhat Green's-function material)

## Summary
The paper takes the static, straight cosmic string (Minkowski in cylindrical coordinates with φ identified modulo 2πA, A < 1, i.e. a 2-cone times the (t,z)-plane) and asks whether the axis obstructs evolution of test fields. Because the metric in Cartesian coordinates is only L^∞ (direction-dependent limits at the axis) and its connection is not L²_loc, Clarke's weak formulation of "generalized hyperbolicity" does not apply. Instead the metric and data are embedded in the Colombeau algebra G; a hierarchy of covariant energy estimates for the mollified metrics (bounded by powers of ε, Lemma 2 by Gronwall) gives existence and uniqueness of a G-solution of the Cauchy problem with H¹×L² data (Thm 5). §5 then shows, via a Volterra equation for the Green's function and an explicit study of limiting null cones (App. C), that the solution is associated with a distribution. Hence the conical spacetime is "G-hyperbolic". The limiting solution has a non-sharp Green's function: it carries a term from scattering off the axis, and the limiting null cone of the mollified metrics is the null cone with the axis included — removing the axis tears it.

## Key points
- [V] Setting: Minkowski in cylindrical coordinates with φ = 0 identified with φ = 2πA, **A < 1** (deficit angle only); static, straight string; the axis Λ is a timelike 2-plane (§1, eq. (1)).
- [V] The holonomy around the axis equals the deficit angle 2π(1−A) and is trivial for "C^{2−}" metrics (citing Wilson–Clarke 1996), so the singularity is a failure of differentiability, not of boundedness: the metric is locally bounded with direction-dependent limits at the axis (§1).
- [V] Geroch–Traschen (as reported here): their regular class forces distributional curvature to be supported on a submanifold of codimension at most one, "which is clearly not the case for the cone" (§1). [S for the GT content itself — see [[geroch1987strings]].]
- [V] The cone does not admit a locally square-integrable connection, so Clarke's (1998) curve-integrable / weak-form generalized hyperbolicity does not apply directly (§1).
- [P] With Colombeau regularization (Clarke–Vickers–Wilson 1996) the energy-momentum density is T⁰₀√−g = T³₃√−g = −2π(1−A)δ⁽²⁾(x¹,x²): a delta source on the axis (§1).
- [V] Definition: G-globally hyperbolic = unique solution of the wave equation in G, associated to a distribution (§1).
- [P] Thm 5 (§4): for metrics of the form −dt² + dz² + g^ε_AB(x^C)dx^A dx^B with |g^ε|, |g_ε^{-1}| ≤ M₀ and |∂^k g^ε| ≤ M_k ε^{-k}, moderate data give a moderate solution and null data a null solution; hence a unique U ∈ G(M). The proof uses that ∇t = 0 (static product structure, eq. (5)) to build a positive, parallel tensor e^{ab}; staticity is essential to the method.
- [P] §5 / App. C: the solution is associated with a distribution, independent of the mollifier; the limiting past null cone C₀⁻(x) has S² topology, the missing piece being generated by geodesics passing within O(ε) of the axis, which in the limit become null lines with a kink at the axis (scattering angles between −α and α, where α is the deficit; App. C).
- [V] Removing the axis "results in a tear in the null cone and destroys the S² topology" (§5).
- [P] The Green's function is not sharp: the solution at x depends on data on C₀⁻(x)∩S and also on C₀⁻(ξ)∩S for ξ ∈ C₀⁻(x)∩Λ — i.e. on causal (piecewise-null) curves that touch the axis (§5, eq. (30) and remark after (33)).
- [V] Conclusion: conical singularities do not disrupt the Cauchy development of test fields on the fixed background; the nonlinear Einstein problem is explicitly not attempted (§6).
- [P] Not treated: excess angle (A > 1), moving or spinning strings, null (BTZ-type, lightlike) singular lines, curved/non-static backgrounds, any causal-curve definition of global hyperbolicity (whole text).
- [P] Citation slip: the reference list gives Geroch–Traschen as "Phys. Rev. D. 38 (1987)"; the KB record (DOI 10.1103/physrevd.36.1017) is volume 36 (references).

## Methods and evidence
- Setting: one explicit metric family (static product of R²_{t,z} with a mollified 2-cone), linear scalar wave equation, H¹×L² data on t = 0.
- Method: Colombeau special/full algebra embedding; hierarchy of covariant energy estimates with ε-power bounds; Gronwall; Sobolev embedding; Green's function via Volterra equation (Friedlander, Bruhat); explicit null-geodesic computation for rotationally symmetric mollifiers.
- Evaluation: mathematical proof; §4 is complete; §5/App. C proves convergence of λ_ε to λ with some steps sketched ("by iteration"), and uses rotationally symmetric kernels (App. A builds a restriction preserving symmetry).
- Artifacts: n/a.

## Limitations and caveats
- Authors concede: no nonlinear (Einstein) analysis; the notion "G-hyperbolicity" differs from Clarke's and the relation is deferred to future work.
- Noticed: the method needs a static product metric with ∇t = 0; nothing transfers to spinning strings (time-helical identification), boosted strings, or BTZ/null lines, where no such parallel timelike field exists near the line.
- Noticed: "G-hyperbolic" is a PDE notion; no causal-curve, Cauchy-surface or J⁺/J⁻ statement is proved. The causal content is implicit in the support of the Green's function.
- Noticed: the Geroch–Traschen codimension statement is relayed, not proved here.

## Contradictions and tensions
- [[geroch1987strings]]: consistent — GT exclude strings; VW go around GT by Colombeau regularization. VW's citation has volume 38 instead of 36.
- [[sbierski2020holonomy]]: VW record that a deficit-angle holonomy is incompatible with a C^{2−} metric; Sbierski's Lemma 2.13 obtains the same incompatibility at C^{0,1}_loc for *unbounded* holonomy. VW's fixed holonomy around arbitrarily short loops is the input the round-1 Lipschitz conjecture needs; VW do not prove anything at Lipschitz regularity.
- Ellis–Schmidt (as quoted in [[hormann2015conical]]) say there are no global Cauchy surfaces once the axis is removed; VW's tear-in-the-null-cone observation is the wave-equation counterpart: the axis must be kept as part of the spacetime for well-posedness. No contradiction, but the two answers to "is the cone globally hyperbolic?" depend on whether the axis is a point set of M.

## Open questions
- Does VW's support of the Green's function equal J⁻(x) ∩ S computed in the cone *with the axis included* and causal curves allowed through the axis? (Reader inference: yes — the extra term is exactly data reached by null curves broken at the axis.)
- Excess angles (A > 1): the energy estimates need |g_ε|, |g_ε^{-1}| bounded, which still holds; the App. C null-cone geometry changes. Untreated.
- Is the uniqueness class (G) physically the same as uniqueness of H¹ distributional solutions? Hörmann notes later H¹ theory still does not cover the cone.

## Relevance to the questions
- (i) Smooth vs causal: everything analytic depends on the smooth structure of R⁴ and a mollified L^∞ metric (energy estimates, Sobolev norms, Green's function, Christoffel symbols, Riemann tensor of g_ε). The only causal content is the domain of dependence: the solution at x depends on data in the past null cone *including* null curves through the axis, and removing the axis destroys the cone's S² topology. That content is purely causal/topological: it says the right causality on the conical spacetime is the one in which the singular line is a set of points and causal curves may pass through it.
- (ii) ETT: ETT's E_α carries exactly this causality (singular line included, causal curves crossing it). VW's "G-hyperbolicity" is therefore *consistent* with ETT's causal global hyperbolicity of E_α [reader inference]: both say the slice t = 0 determines everything, and the dependence domain VW compute (data on C₀⁻(x) plus C₀⁻(ξ) for ξ on the axis) is J⁻(x) ∩ S for the ETT causality. ETT could state the causal half of their result without Colombeau: E_α (static, deficit or excess) is globally hyperbolic with Cauchy surface {t = 0}, and J⁻(x) ∩ {t=0} is compact and equals the closure of the union of straight past light cone pieces and pieces broken at the axis. ETT goes beyond VW in covering excess angles and E_0 (BTZ) lines causally, which VW's method (static product, ∇t = 0) cannot reach.
- (iii) Lean candidate (bridging theorem, causal half): "In the ETT model E_α × R_z (α ∈ (0,∞)), {t = 0} is a Cauchy surface, and for every x, J⁻(x) ∩ {t=0} = (unbroken past cone of x) ∪ ⋃_{ξ ∈ J⁻(x) ∩ Λ} J⁻(ξ) ∩ {t=0}" — the second set is VW's support of the Green's function. A full bridge ("the support of the VW distributional solution is contained in J⁺_ETT(supp data)") is a research target, not a Lean target: it needs Colombeau analysis absent from lean-causal.
