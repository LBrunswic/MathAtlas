# P2 — single-defect circuit $\tau = d^a e\, d^b$

**Verdict: success ✔.** A single difference of two adjacent cyclic rotations compensates
$\tau$ down to a core whose only non-(power-of-$X$) content is $(X^{e-d}-1)$ — gap-sum
exponent $|e-d|$, **independent of $a,b$** (hence of $o=a+b+1$ and $E=(a+b)d+e$). This is
exactly the prize the rationale requested.

## Setup

$\tau = d^a e\, d^b$: length $o=a+b+1$, gap-sum $E=(a+b)d+e$. Its $o$ cyclic rotations are
exactly the words
$$\rho_i \;=\; d^{\,i}\,e\,d^{\,o-1-i},\qquad i=0,\dots,o-1,$$
i.e. the single defect $e$ slides through all $o$ positions. Using the P1 closed form
$S(d^m)=\dfrac{X^{dm}-Y^m}{X^d-Y}$ for the constant runs, the defect contributes a rank-1
correction to the pure circuit:
$$
S(\rho_i) \;=\; S(d^{\,o}) \;+\; (X^{e-d}-1)\,T_i,
\qquad T_i \;=\; \sum_{k=i+1}^{o-1} X^{kd}\,Y^{\,o-1-k}.
$$
The tail $T_i$ is a geometric run, so consecutive rotations telescope to a **single monomial**:
$$
S(\rho_i)-S(\rho_{i+1}) \;=\; (X^{e-d}-1)\,\bigl(T_i-T_{i+1}\bigr)
\;=\; (X^{e-d}-1)\,X^{(i+1)d}\,Y^{\,o-2-i}.
$$

A single consecutive difference therefore carries a factor $Y^{\,o-2-i}$. Powers of $X$ are
harmless ($G$ is odd, coprime to $2$), but powers of $Y$ are **not** ($G$ may share factors
of $3$). The fix: take the **last** consecutive pair $i=o-2$, which kills the $Y$-power
($Y^0=1$).

## Combination

$$
\boxed{\;C_{\text{poly}} \;:=\; S(\rho_{o-2}) - S(\rho_{o-1})
\;=\; X^{(o-2)d}\,(X^{e}-X^{d})
\;=\; X^{(o-1)d}\,(X^{e-d}-1).\;}
$$
Here $\rho_{o-2}=d^{\,o-2}e\,d$ and $\rho_{o-1}=d^{\,o-1}e$. Both are cyclic rotations of
$\tau$, so $G=\gcd_j S^{(j)}(\tau)$ divides their difference. Certified as a polynomial
identity in $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ via `verify_symbolic_identity` over all
$a,b\in[0,5],\,d\in[1,3],\,e\in[1,5]$ (and the degenerate $e=d$ cases, where both sides are $0$).

## Core $C$ and its $X$-exponent

The polynomial core is a pure power of $X$ times the defect factor $(X^{e-d}-1)$. Its only
non-monomial content has $X$-exponent $|e-d|$ — **constant in $a,b$**. Compare the generic
$E=(a+b)d+e\to\infty$: the core's effective exponent does not grow with the family.

At $(X,Y)=(2,3)$: $G \mid 2^{(o-1)d}\,(2^{e-d}-1)$. Since $G$ is odd it is coprime to the
$2$-power, so
$$
G \;\Bigm|\; 2^{\,|e-d|}-1 \qquad(\text{the odd part of }|2^e-2^d|).
$$

## GCD bound

On a genuine cycle $G=|2^E-3^o|$, hence the **a-priori ceiling**
$$
\boxed{\;|2^{E}-3^{o}| \;\le\; 2^{\,|e-d|}-1\;}\qquad(e\neq d).
$$
The bound depends **only on $|e-d|$** — not on $a$, $b$, $o$, or $E$. Since $|2^E-3^o|$ grows
without bound while the right-hand side is fixed by the defect alone, only finitely many
$(a,b)$ can give a cycle for each $(d,e)$ with $e\neq d$ — in fact the ceiling is so small
that for $|e-d|$ fixed it eliminates all but a bounded window of circuit lengths.

## Degenerate locus $C\equiv 0$

$C_{\text{poly}}\equiv 0 \iff X^e=X^d \iff e=d$, i.e. $\tau=d^{o}$ is the **pure circuit**
(pattern P1, no defect). This is the expected single-circuit/bounded-circuit stratum (Steiner
1977), where the cheap ceiling is vacuous and the gcd is governed by P1's
$G=|D|/|2^d-3|$. Off this locus ($e\neq d$) the ceiling bites.

## Caveats / battery results

- **Symbolic identity:** PASS over the grid above (the proof — polynomial equality in
  $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$, including $e<d$, $a=0$, $b=0$).
- **Divisibility battery** at $(2,3)$: PASS over $1512$ instances,
  $a,b\in[0,7],\,d\in[1,4],\,e\in[1,7]$, $e\neq d$. No failures.
- **Ceiling spot-check:** $G \mid 2^{|e-d|}-1$ confirmed directly on sampled instances.
- The combination uses **two rotations and a unit coefficient** — the cheapest possible
  compensation; no monomial twist is even needed for the symbolic identity (the $X$-power sits
  inside the core and is shed by the oddness of $G$ at the integer layer). The strength is that
  choosing the *last* adjacent pair, rather than any other, is what removes the $Y$-growth that
  would otherwise re-introduce $3$-power dependence on $o$.
