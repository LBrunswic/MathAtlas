# Alexander and Thurston norms for word cocycles — a formal account

A Bourbaki-style (definition–theorem–proof) formalization of [[thurston-alexander-norm]]. The algebraic
backbone (the representation $\mu$, the cocycle $S$, the coboundary $D$, the monodromy polynomial) is
recalled from the certified claims **C1–C11** of [`structure.md`](../structure.md); the norms and the
inequality of §4–§5 are new and proved here. The inequality is also guarded by a numerical battery,
[`thurston_alexander_check.py`](thurston_alexander_check.py) (exits 0).

Throughout, all polynomials live over the integral domain $R=\mathbb Z[X,Y]$ and we write
$\langle\phi,v\rangle$ for the pairing of a linear form $\phi$ with a vector $v$. We flag one fact at
the outset because it shapes §5–§6: the quantity $\lVert\phi\rVert_T$ defined below is a function of
the *multiset* of letters of $w$, hence **invariant under permutation of the letters** — it is not
lowered by reordering.

---

## 1. The algebraic structures

**1.1 Definition (grading group, monomials).** Let $H=\mathbb Z^2$ with basis dual to $(X,Y)$; a
monomial $X^aY^b$ has *multidegree* $\deg(X^aY^b)=(a,b)\in H$. The *Newton support* of
$f=\sum_{(a,b)}c_{ab}X^aY^b\in R$ is $\operatorname{supp}(f)=\{(a,b):c_{ab}\ne0\}$ and its *Newton
polytope* is $N(f)=\operatorname{conv}\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset H_{\mathbb R}:=H\otimes\mathbb R$.
A *direction* is a linear form $\phi\in H_{\mathbb R}^{*}$, written $\phi=(\alpha,\beta)$,
$\langle\phi,(a,b)\rangle=\alpha a+\beta b$.

**1.2 Definition (gap words).** Let $A=\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$ (the *gap alphabet*) and $W=A^{*}$ the free
monoid of *gap words* $w=(d_0,\dots,d_{o-1})$, with *length* $o(w)=o$ and *weight* $E(w)=\sum_id_i$.
The *partial sums* are $e_k=\sum_{i<k}d_i$ ($0\le k\le o-1$), so $e_0=0$, $e_{k+1}-e_k=d_k$.

**1.3 Definition (the Magnus–Borel representation).** Let $\mu:W\to GL_2\big(R[Y^{-1}]\big)$ be the
monoid homomorphism determined on letters by $\mu(d)=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}Y&0\\1&X^{d}\end{smallmatrix}\big)$.
By **C1/C2**,
$$\mu(w)=M(w)=\begin{pmatrix}Y^{o}&0\\ S(w)&X^{E}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{o-1}X^{e_k}Y^{\,o-1-k}.$$
The image lies in the Borel (lower-triangular) subgroup; the diagonal carries the two characters
$w\mapsto Y^{o}$ and $w\mapsto X^{E}$, and the off-diagonal entry is the *cocycle* $S$.

**1.4 Definition (cocycle, coboundary).** Let $\chi(w)=X^{E}/Y^{o}$ and $\sigma(w)=S(w)/Y^{o}$. By
**C5**, $\sigma(uv)=\sigma(u)+\chi(u)\sigma(v)$: $\sigma$ is a crossed homomorphism, $\sigma\in
Z^{1}(W,R_\chi)$. By **C6** the coboundary of a constant $c$ is $w\mapsto c\,D(w)$ with the
*augmentation element*
$$D(w)=X^{E}-Y^{o},$$
so $[\sigma]\in H^{1}(W,R_\chi)$ is defined modulo $\langle D\rangle$. We call $D$ the augmentation
because $D(w)=\chi(w)Y^{o}-Y^{o}$ is the image of "$w-1$" under the Borel character (Fox's fundamental
formula); $S$ is the corresponding Fox-derivative datum.

## 2. The Alexander module and the monodromy polynomial

**2.1 Definition (Alexander module of a cyclic word).** Fix $w\in W$ and adjoin the shift variable $t$,
working over $\Lambda=R[Y^{-1}][t]$. The *Alexander module* of $w$ is
$$\mathcal A_w=\operatorname{coker}\!\big(tI-M(w):\ \Lambda^{2}\to\Lambda^{2}\big).$$

**2.2 Proposition (monodromy polynomial = order ideal).** $\mathcal A_w$ is a torsion $\Lambda$-module
with order ideal generated by
$$\Delta_w(t)=\det\!\big(tI-M(w)\big)=(t-X^{E})(t-Y^{o})=t^{2}-(X^{E}+Y^{o})t+X^{E}Y^{o}.$$
*Proof.* For a module with a *square* presentation matrix $P$ over a domain, the order ideal (the
$0$-th Fitting ideal $E_0$) is $(\det P)$. Here
$P=tI-M(w)=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}t-Y^{o}&0\\-S&t-X^{E}\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ is lower-triangular, so
$\det P=(t-Y^{o})(t-X^{E})$. This is exactly **C11**. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**2.3 Remark (fibered analogy).** Rotation of a cyclic word is conjugation by $\mu(u)$ (**C3**), so it
is natural to view $M(w)$ as the monodromy of a mapping torus of the shift and $\Delta_w$ as its
"fibered" Alexander polynomial. We use this only as a guiding picture — no $3$-manifold is constructed,
and (importantly) we do **not** invoke a fibered-face equality below. The eigenvalues are
$\lambda_1=Y^{o}$ (eigenvector $(D,-S)^{\top}$, **C4**) and $\lambda_2=X^{E}$ (eigenvector
$(0,1)^{\top}$), whence $D=\lambda_2-\lambda_1$.

**2.4 Remark (two distinct "Alexander" data — read this before §4).** There are two polynomials with a
claim to the name, living in different gradings, and they must not be conflated:
1. the **monodromy polynomial** $\Delta_w(t)=(t-X^{E})(t-Y^{o})$ of §2.2 — the order ideal of
   $\mathcal A_w$, graded by the shift variable $t$;
2. the **augmentation element** $D=X^{E}-Y^{o}$ — the generator of the coboundary ideal $\langle
   D\rangle$ modulo which the class $[\sigma]\in H^{1}(W,R_\chi)$ is defined (**C6**, §1.4), graded by
   $(X,Y)$ only.
$D$ is the *order datum of the cohomology class*; it is **not** the order ideal of $\mathcal A_w$.
Their only direct link is $D=\lambda_2-\lambda_1$ — the *difference of the roots* of $\Delta_w$, not
$\Delta_w$ itself. The norm theory of §4–§6 is built on $D$ (the class), with $\Delta_w$ recorded for
context in §4.3.

## 3. Newton-polytope seminorms

**3.1 Definition.** For a nonempty finite $P\subset H_{\mathbb R}$ put
$\operatorname{w}_\phi(P)=\max_{p\in P}\langle\phi,p\rangle-\min_{p\in P}\langle\phi,p\rangle$, the
*$\phi$-width* of $P$. For $0\ne f\in R$ set $\operatorname{w}_\phi(f)=\operatorname{w}_\phi(N(f))$.

**3.2 Proposition (seminorm).** For fixed $P$, $\phi\mapsto\operatorname{w}_\phi(P)$ is a seminorm on
$H_{\mathbb R}^{*}$.
*Proof.* $\operatorname{w}_\phi(P)=h_{P}(\phi)+h_{P}(-\phi)$ where $h_P(\phi)=\max_{p\in
P}\langle\phi,p\rangle$ is the support function of $\operatorname{conv}P$. Support functions are
sublinear (positively homogeneous and subadditive); a sum of sublinear functions invariant under
$\phi\mapsto-\phi$ is a seminorm. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**3.3 Proposition (Minkowski additivity).** For $f,g\in R\setminus\{0\}$, $N(fg)=N(f)+N(g)$ (Minkowski
sum), hence $\operatorname{w}_\phi(fg)=\operatorname{w}_\phi(f)+\operatorname{w}_\phi(g)$.
*Proof.* Ostrowski's theorem: over an integral domain the Newton polytope of a product is the Minkowski
sum of the factors' Newton polytopes (the $\phi$-extreme monomials multiply without cancellation because
$R$ is a domain). Support functions are additive under Minkowski sums, and so is width. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 4. The Alexander norm of a word

**4.1 Definition (Alexander norm).** The *Alexander norm* of $w$ in direction $\phi$ is the Newton
width of the augmentation element,
$$\lVert\phi\rVert_{A}(w):=\operatorname{w}_\phi\big(D(w)\big)=\operatorname{w}_\phi(X^{E}-Y^{o}).$$
Here $D$ is taken as the Alexander datum *of the cohomology class* $[\sigma]$: it generates the
coboundary ideal $\langle D\rangle$ modulo which $\sigma$ is defined (**C6**, §1.4), exactly as the
order ideal of an Alexander module measures the ambiguity of its presentation. This is the $(X,Y)$-graded
datum (2) of Remark 2.4, **not** the $t$-graded monodromy polynomial $\Delta_w$; the latter's own width
is recorded in 4.3 for contrast.

**4.2 Proposition (closed form).** $N(D)$ is the segment $[(E,0),(0,o)]$ and
$$\lVert\phi\rVert_{A}(w)=|\alpha E-\beta o|.$$
In the *Collatz direction* $\phi=(\log2,\log3)$, $\lVert\phi\rVert_A(w)=|E\log2-o\log3|=\big|\log(2^{E}/3^{o})\big|$.
*Proof.* $D=X^{E}-Y^{o}$ has support $\{(E,0),(0,o)\}$; its $\phi$-width is
$|\langle\phi,(E,0)-(0,o)\rangle|=|\alpha E-\beta o|$. The specialization is immediate. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**4.3 Proposition (the multivariable Alexander polynomial is a parallelogram).** In the variables
$(X,Y,t)$ with direction $\phi=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$, $N(\Delta_w)$ is the parallelogram with edge
vectors $(E,0,-1)$ and $(0,o,-1)$, and $\operatorname{w}_\phi(\Delta_w)=|\alpha E-\gamma|+|\beta o-\gamma|$.
*Proof.* The four monomials of $\Delta_w$ have exponents $(0,0,2),(E,0,1),(0,o,1),(E,o,0)$; their two
diagonals share the midpoint $(E/2,o/2,1)$, so they form a parallelogram (a $2$-dimensional polytope
embedded in $\mathbb R^{3}$, since the edge vectors $(E,0,-1),(0,o,-1)$ are independent for $E,o\ge1$).
By 3.3 ($\Delta_w$ is the product of the two factors, so $N(\Delta_w)$ is the Minkowski sum of the
segments $N(t-X^{E})$ and $N(t-Y^{o})$) its width is the sum of the edge-widths
$|\alpha E-\gamma|+|\beta o-\gamma|$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Caution.** $\Delta_w$ does **not** reduce to $D$ by setting $\gamma=0$: the direction $(\alpha,\beta,0)$
projects $N(\Delta_w)$ onto the *rectangle* $[0,E]\times[0,o]$ in the $(X,Y)$-plane, of width $\alpha
E+\beta o$ — a sum, not the difference $|\alpha E-\beta o|=\lVert\phi\rVert_A$. The link between
$\Delta_w$ and $D$ is purely $D=\lambda_2-\lambda_1$ (Remark 2.3/2.4), never a face or projection of
$\Delta_w$.

## 5. The Thurston norm of a word

**5.1 Definition (Magnus path; per-letter increments).** The *Magnus path* of $w$ is the sequence of
support points of $S$, $p_k=(e_k,\,o-1-k)\in H$ for $0\le k\le o-1$. Its *increments* are
$\delta_k:=p_{k+1}-p_k=(d_k,-1)$, $0\le k\le o-2$ (from $e_{k+1}-e_k=d_k$).

**5.2 Definition (Thurston norm).** The *Thurston norm* of $w$ in direction $\phi$ is the total
$\phi$-variation accumulated over the letters,
$$\lVert\phi\rVert_{T}(w):=\sum_{k=0}^{o-1}\big|\langle\phi,(d_k,-1)\rangle\big|=\sum_{k=0}^{o-1}|\alpha d_k-\beta|.$$
the sum being over all $o$ letters of the cyclic word (its $o$ "edges"). Geometrically
$\lVert\phi\rVert_T(w)=h_Z(\phi)$, the support function of the *letter zonotope*
$Z=\sum_{k=0}^{o-1}[-\delta_k,\delta_k]$ with $\delta_k=(d_k,-1)$; in particular it is a seminorm in
$\phi$ (3.2 applied to each segment). As a sum over the letters it is a function of the **multiset**
$\{d_0,\dots,d_{o-1}\}$ alone — **invariant under reordering** (the fact flagged in the preamble). The
$o$ terms here are the cyclic edges; the *open* Magnus path of §5.1 has only the $o-1$ increments
$\delta_0,\dots,\delta_{o-2}$, which is the count used in the cocycle bound 6.3.

**5.3 Remark (why "Thurston", and in what sense).** $\lVert\phi\rVert_T$ is the complexity of the cyclic
word as a *singular dual chain*: each letter contributes its $\phi$-defect $|\alpha d_k-\beta|$, the
discrete analogue of $\chi_-$ summed over the pieces of a surface dual to $\phi$. It is the
**no-cancellation (taut-representative) upper model** of a Thurston norm, *not* a minimal complexity:
being reordering-invariant (5.2), it cannot be lowered by re-presenting the word. The genuine,
cancellation-allowing Thurston seminorm — the one McMullen compares to the Alexander norm — is the net
quantity $\lVert\phi\rVert_T^{\mathrm{geom}}$, and the analogy "words are fibered" predicts
$\lVert\phi\rVert_T^{\mathrm{geom}}=\lVert\phi\rVert_A$. We do not establish that identification here;
what §6 proves is the honest two-sided sandwich
$$\lVert\phi\rVert_A\ \le\ \lVert\phi\rVert_T^{\mathrm{geom}}\ \le\ \lVert\phi\rVert_T,$$
where the left side is the cancellation-minimum and the right side our explicit combinatorial bound.

## 6. The Alexander–Thurston inequality

**6.1 Theorem.** For every gap word $w$ and every direction $\phi$,
$$\lVert\phi\rVert_{A}(w)\ \le\ \lVert\phi\rVert_{T}(w),$$
with equality if and only if the nonzero per-letter defects $\alpha d_k-\beta$ ($0\le k\le o-1$) all
share one sign (defects equal to $0$ are allowed); equivalently, the partial sums
$k\mapsto\langle\phi,p_k\rangle$ are monotone.

*Proof.* Write $x_k=\alpha d_k-\beta$. Since $\sum_{k=0}^{o-1}d_k=E$ and the sum has $o$ terms,
$$\sum_{k=0}^{o-1}x_k=\alpha E-\beta o=\langle\phi,(E,0)-(0,o)\rangle,$$
so by 4.2, $\lVert\phi\rVert_A(w)=|\alpha E-\beta o|=\big|\sum_{k}x_k\big|$. The triangle inequality gives
$\big|\sum_k x_k\big|\le\sum_k|x_k|=\lVert\phi\rVert_T(w)$. Equality in the triangle inequality holds iff
all nonzero $x_k$ share a sign. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**6.2 Geometric restatement.** $\lVert\phi\rVert_A\le\lVert\phi\rVert_T$ for all $\phi$ is equivalent to
the inclusion of bodies $N(D)-N(D)\subseteq Z$ (difference body of the Alexander segment inside the
letter zonotope), since the two sides are the support functions of these bodies. The inclusion holds
because the segment's difference vector is $\sum_k\delta_k\in\sum_k[-\delta_k,\delta_k]=Z$.

**6.3 Proposition (cocycle refinement).** The cocycle's own spread is also controlled:
$$\operatorname{w}_\phi\big(S(w)\big)\ \le\ \sum_{k=0}^{o-2}|\alpha d_k-\beta|\ \le\ \lVert\phi\rVert_T(w).$$
*Proof.* With $\psi_k=\langle\phi,p_k\rangle$, $\operatorname{w}_\phi(S)=\max_k\psi_k-\min_k\psi_k$. If
the max/min occur at indices $M,m$ then $|\psi_M-\psi_m|=\big|\sum_{k=m}^{M-1}(\psi_{k+1}-\psi_k)\big|\le
\sum_{k=0}^{o-2}|\psi_{k+1}-\psi_k|=\sum_{k=0}^{o-2}|\alpha d_k-\beta|$, the first $o-1$ terms of
$\lVert\phi\rVert_T$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**6.4 Corollary (equality locus and the oscillation gap).** Both $\lVert\cdot\rVert_A$ and
$\lVert\cdot\rVert_T$ depend only on the multiset of gaps, so reordering changes neither. Equality
$\lVert\phi\rVert_A(w)=\lVert\phi\rVert_T(w)$ holds **iff** the gaps are $\phi$-one-sided (all $d_k$ with
$\alpha d_k>\beta$, or all with $\alpha d_k<\beta$, ties free). When both signs occur the inequality is
strict, with explicit defect
$$\lVert\phi\rVert_T(w)-\lVert\phi\rVert_A(w)=2\!\!\sum_{k:\,\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha d_k-\beta)=-\epsilon}\!\!|\alpha d_k-\beta|,$$
where $\epsilon$ is the sign of $\sum_k(\alpha d_k-\beta)$ — twice the total "wrong-side" imbalance,
i.e. the $\phi$-**oscillation** of the gap sequence.
*Proof.* Write $P=\sum_{x_k\ge0}x_k$, $N=\sum_{x_k<0}|x_k|$ ($x_k=\alpha d_k-\beta$). Then
$\lVert\phi\rVert_T=P+N$ and $\lVert\phi\rVert_A=|P-N|$, so the gap is $2\min(P,N)$, attained on the
smaller side; reordering-invariance is immediate from 5.2. $\qquad\blacksquare$

Thus the *genuine* (cancellation-allowing) Thurston norm equals the net $\lVert\phi\rVert_A$ — the
McMullen "fibered" value — while our combinatorial $\lVert\phi\rVert_T$ overshoots it by exactly the
oscillation $2\min(P,N)$. Equality of the two is the exception, not (as a fibered face would suggest)
the rule; the discrepancy is the price of forbidding cancellation.

**6.5 Example.** Take the Collatz direction $\phi=(\log2,\log3)$, so $\alpha d-\beta=\log(2^{d}/3)$ is
$<0$ for $d=1$ and $>0$ for $d\ge2$.
- *Circuit* $w=(2,2,2)$: all gaps on one side, so by 6.4 equality holds —
  $\lVert\phi\rVert_A=\lVert\phi\rVert_T=3\log\tfrac43\approx0.8630$.
- *Christoffel* $w=(1,2,1,2,2)$ ($E=8,o=5$): gaps straddle $\log_2 3$, so the inequality is strict —
  $\lVert\phi\rVert_A=|8\log2-5\log3|=\log\tfrac{256}{243}\approx0.052$ versus
  $\lVert\phi\rVert_T=2\log\tfrac32+3\log\tfrac43\approx1.674$, an oscillation gap of $4\log\tfrac32
  \approx1.622$ (the two $d=1$ letters are the wrong-side terms, $\epsilon=+$). The tiny
  $\lVert\phi\rVert_A$ records $2^{8}\approx3^{5}$ — this is exactly the $(8,5)$ high-cycle Christoffel
  word with $D=2^{8}-3^{5}=13$: near-balance achieved entirely by cancellation of large per-letter
  imbalances, the structural content of Theorem 6.1.

## 7. Interpretation, scope, and what is open

- **The Collatz reading.** $\lVert\phi\rVert_A(w)=|\log(2^{E}/3^{o})|$ is the *log size barrier*
  ($|D|=|2^{E}-3^{o}|$, the Ellison floor, [[ellison1971-pillai]]); $\lVert\phi\rVert_T(w)=\sum_k|\log(2^{d_k}/3)|$
  is the *total per-step imbalance*. Theorem 6.1 reads: the net imbalance never exceeds the total — a
  cycle's $D$ is small only through cancellation of per-letter imbalances, never because the letters are
  individually balanced.
- **Equality locus.** Equality holds iff all gaps lie on one side of $\beta/\alpha=\log_2 3\approx1.585$,
  i.e. all $d_k=1$ or all $d_k\ge2$. The all-equal circuits $d^{o}$ (the $C=0$ stratum, **P1**) are the
  archetype; they are the $\phi$-monotone (geodesic) representatives where Alexander $=$ Thurston.
- **Span vs. height (do not conflate).** Both norms here are *span* (Newton-width) data; they are
  unrelated to the *height* (Mahler measure $=$ entropy) complexity of [[cocycle-complexity]]. A
  cyclotomic factor inflates a height-complexity of $1$ while contributing fully to the width. The two
  axes meet only on the fibered cone (Fried/McMullen), which for words is the identification
  $\lVert\phi\rVert_T^{\mathrm{geom}}=\lVert\phi\rVert_A$ of §5.3 — an analogy, not established here.
- **Status.** §1–§2 are certified (**C1–C11**); 3.2–3.3 are standard (support functions, Ostrowski);
  4.2, 4.3, 5.2, and Theorem 6.1 with Corollary 6.4 and the cocycle bound 6.3 are proved above; the
  inequality and its equality locus are battery-checked
  ([`thurston_alexander_check.py`](thurston_alexander_check.py), 13728 cases, exit 0). The
  identification $\lVert\phi\rVert_T^{\mathrm{geom}}=\lVert\phi\rVert_A$ (§5.3) is an unproven analogy.
- **Open.** (i) Promote $\lVert\cdot\rVert_A$ to the full multivariable Alexander polynomial $\Delta_w$
  (4.3) and prove a $3$-variable inequality against a Turaev $2$-complex norm of the word's presentation.
  (ii) Extend to the *core* $C(u,v)$: does $\operatorname{w}_\phi(C)$ obey an analogous bound, and where do
  its width and Mahler measure disagree (the place to hunt, per [[thurston-alexander-norm]] §5)?
  (iii) Identify the genuine Thurston seminorm with a $\min$-$\chi_-$ over an explicit cell structure.
