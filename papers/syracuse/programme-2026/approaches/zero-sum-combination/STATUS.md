## Results

- **Literature sweep (done):** there is **no standard term "zero-sum ideal"**, but the object is classical under three names — **augmentation ideal** (kernel of the coefficient-sum map $\varepsilon$, "generated by differences $g-e$", [nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/augmentation+ideal)); **root lattice $A_{p-1}=\{\alpha:\sum\alpha_i=0\}$** with simple-root basis $e_k-e_{k+1}$ (Conway–Sloane / Lie theory); **ideal of differences / lattice ideal** $I_L$ on the value side (Eisenbud–Sturmfels).
- **The statement (proved, [`general-theory.md`](general-theory.md)):** $Z(x_1,\dots,x_p)=\langle x_i-x_j\rangle=\gcd_i(x_i-x_1)\,\mathbb{Z}$ — a principal ideal; minimal generating set is the $p-1$ consecutive differences $x_{k+1}-x_k$ (simple roots). It is the image of the SES $0\to A_{p-1}\to\mathbb{Z}^p\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathbb{Z}\to0$ under evaluation.
- **Structural facts:** translation-invariant ($Z(x_i+c)=Z(x_i)$ — the affine/derivative shadow of the gcd); the ordinary gcd factors as $\gcd(x_i)=\gcd(x_1,\gcd_i(x_i-x_1))$ (base point $\oplus$ differences).
- **Collatz hook:** for rotation sums $S^{(j)}$, the zero-sum combinations $\{\sum c_jS^{(j)}:\sum c_j=0\}=\gcd_{i,j}(S^{(i)}-S^{(j)})\,\mathbb{Z}$ — the compensation regime, now identified as the augmentation ideal of the word's rotation module.
- **Right ring $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ (settled):** the natural setting after evaluating at $(X,Y)=(2,3)$ is $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]=S^{-1}\mathbb{Z}$, $S=\{2^a3^b\}$, which **is a PID — in fact Euclidean**, with norm $\varphi(x)=$ the **$6$-free part** ($x=\pm2^a3^b\varphi(x)$), units $\pm2^a3^b$, primes $p\ge5$. So "stripping powers of $2,3$" $=$ working up to units $=$ the Euclidean norm. $D=2^E-3^o$ is $6$-coprime, so $D\mid S^{(j)}$ and $\gcd_jS^{(j)}=|D|$ transfer unchanged. **Principality of the difference ideal is restored:** $Z(S^{(j)})=\varphi(\gcd_{i,j}(S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}))\,\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$. (Polynomial companion that inverts prefactors: Laurent ring $\mathbb{Z}[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]$ — a UFD, *not* a PID.)
- **Lattice of linear-combination ideals = augmentation-ideal filtration, and it captures cocycles ([`lattice-and-cocycles.md`](lattice-and-cocycles.md), literature sweep done):** the map $L\mapsto\langle\sum\alpha_ix_i:\alpha\in L\rangle$ from sublattices of $\mathbb{Z}^p$ to ideals; the difference step is $L=A_{p-1}$, and iterating it **is** the powers $I^n$ of the augmentation ideal (Passi). "Central" is literal — $D_n(G)=G\cap(1+I^n)$, lower central series, $\gamma_c(F)=(\Delta^c{+}1)\cap F$ (Chen–Fox–Lyndon), graded $=$ free Lie algebra (Jennings–Lazard–Quillen, Jacobi from Hall–Witt). **Cocycles are captured exactly:** Fox calculus $w-1=\sum(\partial w/\partial x_i)(x_i-1)$ makes $\Delta$ free on the differences, and $1$-cocycles $=$ derivations $=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(I,M)$, $H^1=Z^1/B^1$; higher layers $I^n/I^{n+1}$ $=$ Massey/Zassenhaus. **In `cocycle-combine` terms:** $S$ is a $1$-cocycle (off-diagonal of the triangular transfer matrix), the coboundary $cD(w)$ is $B^1$, and the wedge core $C(u,v)=\det\big(\begin{smallmatrix}D&D\\S&S\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ is the cup/bracket of two cocycles with the Plücker$=$Jacobi relation.
- **Full literature review ([`LITERATURE-REVIEW.md`](LITERATURE-REVIEW.md)):** two-part survey + annotated bibliography. Part I (central ideal filtration): augmentation quotients $Q_n$ and **Bachmann–Grünenfelder periodicity**; **dimension subgroup problem** (true $n\le3$, **Rips counterexample at $n=4$**, Gupta); **Lazard N-series**; the associative **lower central series of ideals** $M_k$ (Feigin–Shoikhet–Etingof); finite differences / integer-valued polynomials. Part II (cocycles): Fox calculus; $1$-cocycles $=\mathrm{Hom}(\Delta,M)$; **Magnus cocycle/representation = Gassner/Burau over a Laurent ring — the exact named match for $S$**; cup product $=$ determinant (Suciu–Wang holonomy Lie algebra); Massey/Zassenhaus; Fox subgroup problem. **Key caveat:** dimension-subgroup failure at $n\ge4$, but our usage is degrees $\le2$ (safe). Priority next reads: Gassner/Burau normalization, Passi $Q_n$ bounds, Suciu–Wang determinant formula.
