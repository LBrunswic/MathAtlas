# The lattice of linear-combination ideals, its central filtration, and cocycles

Follow-up to [`general-theory.md`](general-theory.md). The observation: from a family
$x_1,\dots,x_p$ one builds *many* ideals by replacing the $x_i$ with linear combinations;
the difference step $x_i\mapsto x_i-x_j$ produces a "central sub-ideal," and the process
**iterates**, giving a lattice/filtration of ideals. This note records the classical
theory this lands in, and checks the explicit ask: **does that lattice structure capture
cocycle properties?** Answer: **yes — it is exactly the theory whose first layer is "1-cocycles = derivations = $\mathrm{Hom}(I,M)$" (Fox calculus) and whose graded pieces are the higher cocycles.**

## 1. The construction, formalized

Fix a commutative ring $R$ and $x=(x_1,\dots,x_p)\in R^p$. For a **sublattice** $L\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^p$ put
$$
I_L \;=\; \big\langle\, \langle\alpha,x\rangle=\textstyle\sum_i\alpha_i x_i \;:\; \alpha\in L\,\big\rangle\;\subseteq R .
$$
Then $L\mapsto I_L$ is an **order-preserving map from the lattice of sublattices of $\mathbb{Z}^p$ to the lattice of ideals of $R$** ($L\subseteq L'\Rightarrow I_L\subseteq I_{L'}$; it respects sums, $I_{L+L'}=I_L+I_{L'}$). Two landmarks:

- $L=\mathbb{Z}^p\;\Rightarrow\; I_L=\langle x_1,\dots,x_p\rangle=\gcd(x_i)\,\mathbb{Z}$ (over $\mathbb{Z}$).
- $L=A_{p-1}=\ker(\textstyle\sum)\;\Rightarrow\; I_L=\langle x_i-x_j\rangle=\gcd_{i,j}(x_i-x_j)\,\mathbb{Z}$ — the **zero-sum / difference ideal** (the "central sub-ideal").

"Continue the process" = apply the difference/augmentation operator again, getting second differences $(x_i-x_j)-(x_k-x_l)$, etc. This descending tower of ideals is **not ad hoc** — it is the **augmentation-ideal filtration**.

## 2. The iteration is the augmentation-ideal filtration $I\supseteq I^2\supseteq\cdots$

The universal home is the group ring. For the free abelian model $R'=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^p]=\mathbb{Z}[t_1^{\pm},\dots,t_p^{\pm}]$, the **augmentation map** $\varepsilon(t_i)=1$ has kernel $I=\Delta$ generated by the $(t_i-1)$, and the tower of "iterated differences" is precisely the **powers** $I^n$. Facts:

- **Finite-difference instance ($p=1$):** $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}]=\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}]$, $I=(t-1)$, and the forward-difference operator $\Delta f=f(t{+}1)-f(t)$ **is multiplication by $(t-1)$**; $I^n=(t-1)^n$. The dual objects are the **integer-valued polynomials** $\mathrm{Int}(\mathbb{Z})=\{f\in\mathbb{Q}[x]:f(\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\}$, free on the **binomial basis** $\binom{x}{k}$ (Pólya–Ostrowski; Cahen–Chabert). So "a sequence is a degree-$\lt n$ polynomial $\iff$ its $n$-th difference vanishes" is the statement $\Delta^n\in I^n$.
- **Passi functors:** $P_n(G)=I^n/I^{n+1}$ and "$kG/I^{n+1}$" classify **polynomial maps of degree $\le n$** out of $G$; $Q_1\cong G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and for abelian $G$, $Q_2\cong\mathrm{Sym}^2(G)$ (Passi). [arXiv:2309.07605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07605), [arXiv:0707.0371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.0371), [Fox quotients arXiv:0707.0281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.0281).
- Reference: **I. B. S. Passi, _Group Rings and Their Augmentation Ideals_** (Springer LNM 715) — the canonical text for this filtration.

## 3. "Central" is literal — dimension subgroups & lower central series

The user's word "central" is exact: the augmentation powers cut out the **dimension subgroups**
$$
D_n(G)=G\cap(1+I^n),\qquad \gamma_n(G)\subseteq D_n(G),
$$
and these approximate the **lower central series** $\gamma_n$. For a **free** group $F$ the two coincide — **Chen–Fox–Lyndon / Magnus:** $\gamma_c(F)=(\Delta^c+1)\cap F$. The associated graded is governed by **Jennings–Lazard–Quillen**: $\mathrm{gr}(kG)\cong U(\mathrm{gr}_{\mathrm{lcs}}G)$ (graded universal enveloping algebra), a **free Lie algebra** in the free case (Magnus / Zassenhaus Lie algebra), the **Jacobi identity coming from the Hall–Witt commutator identity**. [arXiv:1601.08006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.08006), [Zassenhaus↔Magnus arXiv:2511.13379](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13379), [Passi, _Dimension Subgroups_](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-85818-8_2).

So: **the iterated-difference ideal lattice is the lower-central / dimension filtration realized inside the ideal lattice.**

## 4. Cocycles ARE captured — Fox calculus and the graded Lie algebra

This is the requested check. The bridge is **Fox free differential calculus** (Fox, _Annals_ 1953).

- **Fundamental formula.** For $w$ in the free group on $x_1,\dots,x_n$, in $\mathbb{Z}F$:
$$
w-1=\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\partial w}{\partial x_i}\,(x_i-1).
$$
Hence the augmentation ideal $\Delta_F$ is a **free left $\mathbb{Z}F$-module on the differences $(x_i-1)$** — literally "the ideal generated by differences," now with a basis. ([Fox derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_derivative).)
- **1-cocycles = derivations = crossed homomorphisms.** A derivation satisfies $D(uv)=D(u)+u\!\cdot\!D(v)$, equivalently the **cocycle identity** $c(gh)=c(g)+g\!\cdot\!c(h)$. The Fox derivatives $\partial/\partial x_i$ are the free generators of derivations, and
$$
\mathrm{Der}(G,M)\;\cong\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(I,M),\qquad H^1(G,M)=\mathrm{Der}/\mathrm{PDer}.
$$
**So 1-cocycles are exactly the $\mathbb{Z}G$-module maps out of the first augmentation layer $I=I^1$.** ([crossed homomorphism, nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/crossed+homomorphism); [Encyclopedia of Math](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Crossed_homomorphism).)
- **Triangular-representation form.** $g\mapsto\big(\begin{smallmatrix}g&c(g)\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ is a homomorphism **iff** the off-diagonal $c$ is a 1-cocycle. ([Lie-algebra cohomology notes, Wagemann](https://www.math.sciences.univ-nantes.fr/~wagemann/LAlecture.pdf).)
- **Higher cocycles = higher layers.** The bar resolution is built from $I^{\otimes n}$; the graded pieces $I^n/I^{n+1}$ of the augmentation filtration carry the **Massey-product / Zassenhaus** structure (generalized Bockstein), i.e. higher cohomology operations. [arXiv:2004.11510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.11510), [Zassenhaus filtration & Massey products arXiv:1301.0896](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.0896).

**Verdict:** the lattice/filtration structure does not merely *touch* cocycles — its layers **are** the cocycle theory ($H^1$ at level 1, Massey/cup at higher levels).

## 5. Mapping it onto `cocycle-combine` (does our $S$ fit?)

It fits exactly, and renames our objects:

- **$S$ is a 1-cocycle.** The transfer matrix $M(w)=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}Y^{o}&0\\ S(w)&X^{E}\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ is a monoid hom, so $M(uv)=M(u)M(v)$ gives
$$
S(uv)=S(u)\,Y^{o_v}+X^{E_u}\,S(w)\big|_{w=v}=S(u)Y^{o_v}+X^{E_u}S(v).
$$
That is the crossed-homomorphism identity (§4) for the word monoid acting on $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ via $u\cdot(-)=X^{E_u}(-)$, twisted by $Y^{o}$ — $S$ is the **off-diagonal of a triangular representation**, a 1-cocycle.
- **Coboundaries.** The structure note's "coboundary of a constant $c$ is $w\mapsto cD(w)$" is precisely $B^1$, and "$S$ defined mod $\langle D\rangle$" is $H^1=Z^1/B^1$.
- **The wedge core is a bracket of two cocycles.** $C(u,v)=D(u)S(v)-D(v)S(u)=\det\big(\begin{smallmatrix}D(u)&D(v)\\ S(u)&S(v)\end{smallmatrix}\big)$ is **antisymmetric and bilinear in the cocycle data** — the cup-product/Lie-bracket on the associated graded — and the already-noted **Plücker/Jacobi relation $\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}D(u)C(v,w)=0$ is the Jacobi identity** of that graded Lie algebra (the Hall–Witt fingerprint of §3). The rotations/differences $S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}$ are the difference operator of §1–2.

So `cocycle-combine` is, in this language, computing in **$H^1$ and the associated graded Lie algebra of the augmentation ideal of the word monoid** — the user's iterated-difference ideal lattice.

## 6. What this buys, and what's open

- **Reframing (free):** the wedge core $C(u,v)$ = cup/bracket of 1-cocycles; its Jacobi relation = Lie identity. The compensation search lives in $H^1$ + graded layers.
- **Possible leverage (open):** **Passi's structure theorems for augmentation quotients $I^n/I^{n+1}$** are exactly bounds on the graded layers — could they bound $\varphi\big(\gcd_{i,j}(S^{(i)}-S^{(j)})\big)$ a priori (the $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ generator of §`general-theory`)? Unknown; worth a dedicated look.
- **Literature to mine next:** Passi, _Group Rings and Their Augmentation Ideals_ (LNM 715); N. Gupta, _Free Group Rings_; Fox's five _Annals_ papers (1953–60); Quillen, "On the associated graded ring of a group ring"; Mináč–Tân et al. on Massey products & the Zassenhaus filtration.
