# Rationale — zero-sum-combination

## Idea

It is a standard result that the $\mathbb{Z}$-ideal generated by integers $x_1,\dots,x_p$ is
$$\langle x_1,\dots,x_p\rangle = \gcd(x_1,\dots,x_p)\,\mathbb{Z}.$$

This approach asks the analogous question for the **zero-sum** (or **balanced**) combinations: what can we say about the set
$$
Z(x_1,\dots,x_p) \;=\; \Big\{\, \alpha_1 x_1 + \cdots + \alpha_p x_p \;:\; \alpha_i \in \mathbb{Z},\ \sum_{i=1}^p \alpha_i = 0 \,\Big\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}.
$$

We constrain the coefficient vector $(\alpha_i)$ to lie in the hyperplane $\sum_i\alpha_i=0$ instead of ranging over all of $\mathbb{Z}^p$. The questions: is $Z$ a subgroup / ideal? What is its generator? How does it relate to $\gcd(x_1,\dots,x_p)$ and to the pairwise differences $x_i-x_j$?

## Why it might work

This is a **foundational** approach: the goal is a clean, iron-clad statement of the zero-sum-ideal fact, stated and proved once, then reused across the other approaches — the same role the `diophantine` approach plays for the closed-form identity. It is not tied to a single Collatz application.

Its most obvious consumer is the rotation-sum cluster (`cycle-gcd`, `cocycle-combine`, `circulant_matrix`): on a cycle the $o$ cyclic-rotation sums $S^{(0)},\dots,S^{(o-1)}$ are all divisible by $D=2^E-3^o$, and the leverage there comes from forming integer combinations $\sum_j c_j S^{(j)}$ that compensate to something small. The zero-sum constraint $\sum_j c_j=0$ is exactly the regime that kills the common ($D$-divisible) part and isolates the differences $S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}$ — so the ideal of zero-sum combinations of a tuple is the abstract skeleton of the compensation search. But the statement is application-agnostic and worth having on its own terms.

## Known prior art / why it isn't obviously dead

The plain-ideal fact ($\langle x_i\rangle=\gcd\cdot\mathbb{Z}$, Bézout) is textbook. The zero-sum variant is elementary commutative algebra and is presumably classical, but the value of a foundational entry is precisely to pin the exact statement, its generator, and its proof in one citable place so downstream approaches can cite rather than re-derive.
