# General theory of the "zero-sum ideal"

Foundational note for the `zero-sum-combination` approach. Goal: pin the exact
statement, its standard names in the literature, and the proof, so downstream
approaches can cite rather than re-derive.

Notation. Fix integers $x_1,\dots,x_p$ and set
$$
Z(x_1,\dots,x_p)\;=\;\Big\{\,\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^p \alpha_i x_i \;:\; \alpha_i\in\mathbb{Z},\ \sum_i \alpha_i=0\,\Big\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}.
$$

## TL;DR — the answer

$$
\boxed{\,Z(x_1,\dots,x_p)\;=\;\big\langle\, x_i-x_j \;:\; 1\le i\lt j\le p\,\big\rangle\;=\;\gcd\big(x_2-x_1,\;x_3-x_1,\;\dots,\;x_p-x_1\big)\,\mathbb{Z}.}
$$

So it **is** a subgroup (indeed a principal ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$), and its generator is the **gcd of the pairwise differences**. Equivalently it is the image of the *augmentation ideal* / *root lattice $A_{p-1}$* under the evaluation map. Three structural facts:

- It is **translation-invariant**: $Z(x_1+c,\dots,x_p+c)=Z(x_1,\dots,x_p)$ for every $c$. It is the largest translation-invariant ideal "inside" the data — the *affine/derivative* shadow of the ordinary gcd.
- The ordinary gcd factors through it: $\;\gcd(x_1,\dots,x_p)=\gcd\!\big(x_1,\ \gcd_j(x_j-x_1)\big)$. (Base point $\oplus$ differences.)
- $Z=\mathbb{Z}$ (the whole group) $\iff$ the $x_i$ are not all congruent mod any prime, i.e. $\gcd$ of differences $=1$; $Z=\{0\}\iff$ all $x_i$ equal.

## The object has three standard names

Searching turned up **no standard term "zero-sum ideal."** But the object is completely classical and shows up under three names, depending on whether you look at the *coefficient* side $\{\alpha:\sum\alpha_i=0\}$ or the *value* side $\{\sum\alpha_i x_i\}$.

### 1. Augmentation ideal (the canonical name)

The map $\varepsilon:\mathbb{Z}^p\to\mathbb{Z}$, $\varepsilon(\alpha)=\sum_i\alpha_i$, is the **augmentation map** (the coefficient-sum map of a group/monoid algebra). Its kernel is the **augmentation ideal**, and the standard structural fact is exactly what we need:

> The augmentation ideal is the kernel of $\varepsilon$ — "those formal linear combinations whose coefficients sum to zero" — and it is **generated by differences of generators**: it is "free on $\{g-e: g\neq e\}$," with relations $g_1(g_2-e)=(g_1g_2-e)-(g_1-e)$.
> — [nLab, *augmentation ideal*](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/augmentation+ideal)

For us the "group elements" are the basis vectors $e_1,\dots,e_p$ of $\mathbb{Z}^p$, so $\ker\varepsilon$ is generated by $\{e_i-e_1\}$ (equivalently $\{e_i-e_j\}$). Our $Z$ is the image of $\ker\varepsilon$ under evaluation $e_i\mapsto x_i$.

### 2. Root lattice $A_{p-1}$ (the lattice / Lie-theory name)

On the coefficient side, $\ker\varepsilon=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^p:\sum_i\alpha_i=0\}$ is verbatim the definition of the **root lattice of type $A_{p-1}$**:
$$
A_{p-1}=\Big\{(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_p)\in\mathbb{Z}^p:\ \textstyle\sum_i\alpha_i=0\Big\},
$$
with roots $\pm(e_i-e_j)$ and a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of **simple roots** $\alpha_k=e_k-e_{k+1}$, $k=1,\dots,p-1$ (Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*; standard in Lie theory — e.g. [arXiv:1811.12176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.12176), [arXiv:2304.10186](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10186)). This pins the minimal generating set: $p-1$ consecutive differences $x_{k+1}-x_k$ already generate $Z$ — you never need all $\binom{p}{2}$ differences.

The whole thing is the short exact sequence
$$
0\;\longrightarrow\;A_{p-1}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{Z}^p\;\xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ }\;\mathbb{Z}\;\longrightarrow\;0,
$$
and $Z=x_\ast(A_{p-1})$ where $x_\ast:\mathbb{Z}^p\to\mathbb{Z}$ is evaluation at $(x_i)$.

### 3. Ideal of differences / lattice ideal (the value / toric name)

In a general commutative ring $R$ with elements $x_1,\dots,x_p$, the same construction gives the **ideal of differences** $\langle x_i-x_j\rangle$. In the binomial/toric world this is the additive shadow of the **lattice ideal** $I_L=\langle x^{u}-x^{v}: u-v\in L\rangle$ attached to the lattice $L=\ker\varepsilon$ (Eisenbud–Sturmfels, *Binomial Ideals*, [arXiv:alg-geom/9401001](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9401001)). For the Collatz application the ring is $\mathbb{Z}$ (a PID), so the ideal is principal and the gcd description is exact.

## Proof over $\mathbb{Z}$ (short, self-contained)

Both inclusions of $Z=\langle x_i-x_j\rangle$:

- **($\supseteq$)** Each difference $x_i-x_j=1\cdot x_i+(-1)\cdot x_j$ has coefficient sum $0$, so $x_i-x_j\in Z$; hence $\langle x_i-x_j\rangle\subseteq Z$ (and $Z$ is closed under $\mathbb{Z}$-combinations, being a subgroup).
- **($\subseteq$)** If $\sum_i\alpha_i=0$ then $\alpha_1=-\sum_{i\ge2}\alpha_i$, so
$$
\sum_i\alpha_i x_i=\sum_{i\ge2}\alpha_i x_i+\alpha_1 x_1=\sum_{i\ge2}\alpha_i(x_i-x_1)\in\langle x_i-x_1\rangle.
$$

Then $\langle x_i-x_j\rangle=\langle x_i-x_1\rangle$ (since $x_i-x_j=(x_i-x_1)-(x_j-x_1)$), and in the PID $\mathbb{Z}$ this principal-ideal generator is $\gcd_i(x_i-x_1)=\gcd$ of all pairwise differences (every difference lies in $\gcd_i(x_i-x_1)\,\mathbb{Z}$, and Bézout realizes the gcd). $\square$

**Translation invariance** is immediate from the constraint: $\sum_i\alpha_i(x_i+c)=\sum_i\alpha_i x_i+c\sum_i\alpha_i=\sum_i\alpha_i x_i$. **gcd factorization**: $\langle x_1,\dots,x_p\rangle=\langle x_1\rangle+\langle x_i-x_1\rangle$ gives $\gcd(x_i)=\gcd(x_1,\ \gcd_i(x_i-x_1))$.

## The right ring: $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ (a PID — so principality is restored after evaluation)

The caveat above ("$\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ is not a PID") evaporates in the ring we actually care about. Evaluate the rotation sums at $(X,Y)=(2,3)$ and sit in
$$
\mathbb{Z}[1/6]=\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,\tfrac13]=S^{-1}\mathbb{Z},\qquad S=\{2^a3^b:a,b\ge0\},
$$
the rationals whose denominators are products of $2$ and $3$.

**$\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ is a PID — in fact Euclidean.**

- *PID:* a localization of a PID is a PID. Every ideal $J\subseteq S^{-1}\mathbb{Z}$ equals $S^{-1}(J\cap\mathbb{Z})$, and $J\cap\mathbb{Z}=(a)$ is principal in $\mathbb{Z}$, so $J=(a/1)$.
- *Euclidean, with the "strip the $2$s and $3$s" norm:* every nonzero $x$ factors uniquely as $x=\pm2^a3^b\,m$ with $m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\gt0}$ coprime to $6$; set $\varphi(x)=m$, the **$6$-free part**. Given $x,y\neq0$ with $6$-free parts $m,n$, integer division $m=sn+t$ ($0\le t\lt n$) and absorbing the unit yields a remainder $r$ with $\varphi(r)\le t\lt n=\varphi(y)$ (or $r=0$). So $\varphi$ is a Euclidean function.
- *Units:* $(\mathbb{Z}[1/6])^\times=\{\pm2^a3^b:a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\}$. *Primes:* exactly $p\ge5$.

**Why this is the natural home.** Two structural facts line up:

1. **"Stripping powers of $2$ and $3$" = working up to units of $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$.** The monomial prefactors $X^aY^b=2^a3^b$ that distinguish rotation sums (and appear in every increment/telescoping core in `cocycle-combine`) are precisely the units; the Euclidean norm $\varphi$ is the canonical name for "the part that survives stripping." This is exactly the structure note's "$S$ defined mod $\langle D\rangle$, ignores prefactors," P2's "odd part $2^{|e-d|}-1$," P11's "unit-stripped size" — all *up to units of $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$*.
2. **$D=2^E-3^o$ is coprime to $6$** (mod $2$: $\equiv1$; mod $3$: $\equiv(-1)^E\not\equiv0$), so it is a genuine non-unit supported on primes $p\ge5$, and $|D|$ is already its own $6$-free part. Hence $D\mid S^{(j)}$ and the exact-gcd theorem $\gcd_jS^{(j)}=|D|$ transfer to $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ **unchanged** — inverting $2,3$ loses nothing about $D$.

**Payoff (principal difference-ideal for the rotation sums).** Back in a PID, the foundational theorem applies verbatim to the integer tuple $(S^{(0)},\dots,S^{(o-1)})$ viewed in $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$:
$$
Z\big(S^{(0)},\dots,S^{(o-1)}\big)=\big\langle S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}\big\rangle=\varphi\!\Big(\gcd_{i,j}\big(S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}\big)\Big)\,\mathbb{Z}[1/6],
$$
a principal ideal generated by the **$6$-free part of the gcd of differences**.

**Two layers, kept distinct.** $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ is the home for the *number-theoretic gcd statement at a fixed word*. The $\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ layer in `cocycle-combine` exists for the *different* purpose of cores uniform across a word family ($q$-free, $(a,b)$-free), which evaluation destroys. The polynomial-level companion that inverts the prefactors is the **Laurent ring $\mathbb{Z}[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}]$** (units $=$ monomials, matching "strip prefactors") — a UFD but *not* a PID; the PID property is specifically the specialization $(X,Y)\mapsto(2,3)$.

## Why this matters here (intended consumer)

The natural application is the rotation-sum cluster (`cycle-gcd`, `cocycle-combine`, `circulant_matrix`). There the tuple is the $o$ cyclic rotation sums $S^{(0)},\dots,S^{(o-1)}$ of a gap word, all divisible by $D=2^E-3^o$ on a cycle. This note says:

- The **zero-sum combinations** $\{\sum_j c_j S^{(j)}:\sum_j c_j=0\}$ are exactly the ideal generated by the differences $S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}$, i.e. (in $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$) the principal ideal $\varphi(\gcd_{i,j}(S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}))\,\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ — the regime the compensation search lives in, now identified abstractly.
- On a cycle every $S^{(j)}\equiv0\pmod D$, so all differences are $\equiv0\pmod D$; the *full* gcd splits as $\gcd_jS^{(j)}=\gcd(S^{(0)},\ \gcd_{i,j}(S^{(i)}-S^{(j)}))$, consistent with the exact-gcd result $\gcd_jS^{(j)}=|D|$ (and $|D|$ is $6$-coprime, so it equals its own $\varphi$).
- It is the **augmentation ideal** of the rotation $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/o]$-module of the word — which is presumably the right home for the cocycle/transfer-matrix algebra already in `cocycle-combine/structure`.
