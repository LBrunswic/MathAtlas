# The scheme picture, reviewed, and where the known patterns sit

*2026-10-30. Review and extension of [`RATIONALE.md`](RATIONALE.md) §1–§5. Math uses `\lt`/`\gt`.
New script: [`budget.py`](budget.py). Status labels are used throughout:
- **proved** — in Lean, axiom-clean;
- **conditional** — proved in Lean from a stated `Prop`;
- **derived here** — paper argument, not formalized;
- **checked** — numerical.*

---

## 0. Summary

1. **One identity carries all of it.** A cycle is a section $\sigma$ of the torus
   $\mathbb T=\mathbb G_m^2$ over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[1/6]$ that meets the incidence
   scheme $\mathrm{Triv}_w$ exactly as much as it meets the divisor $K_w$. By the product formula,
   "as much as" is measured at ∞:
   $$\text{cycle}\iff(\sigma\cdot\mathrm{Triv}_w)_{\mathrm{fin}}=(\sigma\cdot K_w)_{\mathrm{fin}}=\log|D_w(\sigma)|_\infty .$$
   The defect is $\log k_w$, where $k_w=|D|/\gcd(D,S)$ is the $3x+k$ index of $w$.
2. **A family is a subgroup orbit in a bigger torus.** Its pattern exclusions are a single height
   inequality:
   - non-archimedean side: *cores*, which are elimination ideals of the incidence variety onto
     quotient tori;
   - archimedean side: the tropicalisation of $D$.
3. **RATIONALE's dichotomy, "r = 1 ⇒ core, r ≥ 2 ⇒ Vojta", is too coarse.** It looks only at
   *full* cores, i.e. elimination onto a point. Families with $r\ge2$ have **partial cores**: one
   rotation core per block, each depending only on the *other* blocks. These settle many $r\ge2$
   families with Baker/Matveev alone.
4. **The tropical budget (derived here, checked).** For a family of $k$ independent blocks, the
   rotation cores suffice, off their loci and conditional on Matveev, iff
   $$\min(A,B)\lt1,\qquad A=\sum_{\text{above}}\Big(1-\tfrac{\log Q_j}{\log P_j}\Big),\quad B=\sum_{\text{below}}\Big(1-\tfrac{\log P_j}{\log Q_j}\Big),$$
   with $P=2^{|u|}$ and $Q=3^{\mathrm{len}\,u}$.
   - It contains, as special cases, WPow ($k=1$), TwoExp (same side: $A=0$ or $B=0$) and Straddle
     ($k=2$).
   - In the parity alphabet it reproduces exactly the reach of Baker-type arguments on $m$-cycles:
     $m\le2$, i.e. Steiner and Simons.
5. **Each known pattern is one geometric situation.** §3 gives the dictionary:
   - full core: constant ceiling;
   - divisorial component: powers of a cycle;
   - partial cores within budget: Baker suffices;
   - outside the budget: Vojta/Levin;
   - unit core: Knight's balanced words.
6. **A correction.** `Straddle.lean` and `PATTERNS.md` §6 said "two independent exponents is the
   limit of this method". That is **wrong**. The correct threshold is the budget above, e.g.
   $1^a\,2^b\,1^c\,2^d$ is inside it. Both are corrected in this commit.

## 1. The objects

### 1.1 Representation and cocycle

- **The monoid.** It is the free monoid $F$ on the two Syracuse branches $a(z)=(3z+1)/2$ and
  $b(z)=z/2$ (parity alphabet $\{1,0\}$). The submonoid of gap words is generated by
  $d\mapsto 1\,0^{d-1}$.
- **The representation.** Over $A=\mathbb Z[X^{\pm},Y^{\pm}]$ it is
  $\rho:F\to\mathrm{Aff}_1(A)$, with $\rho(w)(T)=(Y^{o}T+S_w)/X^{E}$, where $E=|w|$ and
  $o=\mathrm{len}\,w$. Specialising at $(X,Y)=(2,3)$ gives the Syracuse dynamics.
- **The cocycle.** $S$ is a $(\chi_X,\chi_Y)$-twisted cocycle:
  $S_{uv}=Y^{\mathrm{len}\,v}S_u+X^{|u|}S_v$ (`S_append`). The coboundaries are $c\,D$, with
  $D_w=X^{E}-Y^{o}$ (`coboundary`, `isSCocycle_D`).
- **One word.** For the cyclic submonoid, $H^1(\langle w\rangle;A_\chi)=A/(D_w)$, and the residue
  of $[S]$ is $S_w\bmod D_w$. Over $\operatorname{Frac}A$, $[S]$ restricts to a coboundary on every
  $\langle w\rangle$, with $c=x^\ast_w=S_w/D_w$: the fixed point.

### 1.2 Torus, section, divisor, incidence

- **Torus and section.** $\mathbb T=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[X^\pm,Y^\pm]$, and the Syracuse
  section is $\sigma=(2,3):\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[1/6]\to\mathbb T$. Inverting exactly 2 and
  3 is forced, because the character group of $F$ has rank 2 (RATIONALE §1).
- **The divisor.** $K_w=V(D_w)=\ker(\chi_w)$ with $\chi_w=X^EY^{-o}$. This is a **subgroup
  scheme**: an extension of $\mu_{\gcd(E,o)}$ by $\mathbb G_m$.
- **The incidence scheme.** $\mathrm{Triv}_w=V(D_w,S_w)\subset K_w$ is the locus where $\rho(w)$
  is trivial, i.e. where it has a fixed point at every value of $T$. It depends only on the
  conjugacy class of $w$.
- **The fixed-point map.** $x^\ast_w=S_w/D_w:\mathbb T\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^1$. A cycle is
  $x^\ast_w(\sigma)\in\mathbb Z$.

### 1.3 The cycle condition as an intersection identity

- **The finite scheme.** $\sigma^{-1}(K_w)=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z/D_w(2,3)$. It records the
  primes where the Syracuse point lies on $K_w$, with multiplicity.
- **Equivalent conditions.** The following are equivalent:
  - $w$ is a cycle word, i.e. $D\mid S$;
  - $\sigma^{-1}(\mathrm{Triv}_w)=\sigma^{-1}(K_w)$ as schemes;
  - $(\sigma\cdot\mathrm{Triv}_w)_{\mathrm{fin}}=\sum_p v_p(\gcd(D,S))\log p$ equals
    $(\sigma\cdot K_w)_{\mathrm{fin}}=\log|D|$.
- **The product formula, which is "combining all places".**
  - $D$ is a unit at 2 and 3, so the places in $S$ contribute nothing.
  - $(\sigma\cdot K_w)_{\mathrm{fin}}=\log|D(\sigma)|_\infty$: the *finite* intersection with the
    divisor is the *archimedean* size of $D$.
  - So a cycle is the statement that the codimension-2 intersection
    $\sigma\cdot\mathrm{Triv}_w$ is as large as the archimedean height allows.
- **The defect.** $\delta_w=\log|D|-\log\gcd(D,S)=\log k_w\ge0$, where $k_w$ is the least $k$ for
  which $w$ is a $3x+k$ cycle (RATIONALE §2).
  - Cycle iff $\delta_w=0$.
  - The $3x+k$ barrier says exactly that $\delta_w$ is invisible over $\mathbb Z[1/6k]$.
- **Silverman's gcd.** $\log\gcd(D,S)$ is the height of $\sigma$ relative to the exceptional
  divisor of $\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathrm{Triv}_w}\mathbb T$. That is the hook to Vojta.

### 1.4 What each place does in an exclusion

| place | role | input |
|---|---|---|
| 2, 3 | run coordinates $2^{\cdot},3^{\cdot}$ are units; the factor $X^{a\vert w\vert}$ in every core identity is harmless | $D$ coprime to 6 (`isCoprime_Dz_six`) |
| $p\mid D$ | divisibility: $D\mid S$ forces $D\mid$ any element of the ideal $(D,S)$, i.e. any **core** | elimination (resultants, rotation cores) |
| ∞ | size: $\vert D\vert$ is large, cores are small | growth (no Baker) or Matveev near the critical slope |

## 2. Families

### 2.1 A family is a subgroup orbit

- **The family.** Take $W(a)=u_1^{a_1}x_1\cdots u_k^{a_k}x_k$ with independent $a\in\mathbb N^k$.
  Its *run torus* is $\mathbb T_F=\mathbb T\times\mathbb G_m^{2k}$, with coordinates
  $z_j=X^{a_j|u_j|}$ and $s_j=Y^{a_j\mathrm{len}u_j}$.
- **Polynomial forms.** $D_W$ and $\mu S_W$ are Laurent polynomials $\tilde D,\tilde S$ on
  $\mathbb T_F$, by the closed forms `Family.closed` and `wpow_rel`. Here
  $\mu=\prod(X^{|u_j|}-Y^{\mathrm{len}u_j})$.
- **Points.** Members are the points
  $\sigma_a=(2,3,2^{a_1|u_1|},3^{a_1\mathrm{len}u_1},\dots)$. They form a translate of a finitely
  generated subgroup $\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z^k$ of $\mathbb T_F(\mathbb Z[1/6])$.
- **The question.** For which $a$ does $\sigma_a^{-1}\mathcal V_F=\sigma_a^{-1}\tilde K$, where
  $\mathcal V_F=V(\tilde D,\tilde S)$?

Dependent exponents put the points on a subtorus. One shared exponent, as in $r=1$ families and
skeletons, gives a 1-dimensional orbit. **Multiplicative exponents** (towers) are not a subgroup
orbit at all; see §3.

### 2.2 Cores are elimination ideals, full or partial

- **Full core.** $(\tilde D,\tilde S)\cap\mathbb Z[X^\pm,Y^\pm]$, i.e. elimination of every run
  coordinate. It is nonzero iff $\mathcal V_F\to\mathbb T$ is not dominant (RATIONALE §3). That
  happens for one parameter with equal slopes ($r=1$), and then it is a resultant.
- **Partial cores.**
  - For a quotient torus $\pi_I:\mathbb T_F\to\mathbb T_{F,I}$ that forgets the blocks in $I$, a
    partial core is $(\tilde D,\tilde S)\cap\mathcal O(\mathbb T_{F,I})$. It is nonzero iff
    $\pi_I(\mathcal V_F)$ is not dominant.
  - The **rotation lemma** gives one explicitly for each block. Rotating $W$ to
    $u_i^{a_i}\,\mathrm{rest}_i$, the identity
    $D(u)S(u^aw')-S(u)D(u^aw')=X^{a|u|}C(u,w')$ (`wpow_append_identity`) puts
    $C(u_i,\mathrm{rest}_i)$ in the ideal, and it does not involve $a_i$.
  - So $\pi_{\{i\}}(\mathcal V_F)\subseteq V(C(u_i,\mathrm{rest}_i))$ for every $i$.
- **Where the $r\ge2$ statement needs refining.** RATIONALE's "$r\ge2$ ⇒ no core" is true of full
  cores, and false of partial ones.
- **Geometry of a core.** $V(C(u,v))$ is where $\rho(u)$ and $\rho(v)$ share their finite fixed
  point; $C$ is the translation part of the commutator.

### 2.3 Archimedean tropicalisation

At ∞, a Laurent polynomial evaluated at a subgroup point has the size of its largest monomial.

- **Upper bound, always valid.**
  $\log|f(\sigma_a)|\le\operatorname{trop}_\infty f(a)+\log\#f$, where
  $\operatorname{trop}_\infty f(a)=\max_m\big(\log|c_m|+\langle m,\log\sigma_a\rangle\big)$ is
  piecewise linear in $a$.
- **Lower bound, for $D$ only.**
  $\log|D(\sigma_a)|\ge\operatorname{trop}_\infty D(a)-O(\log|a|)$, with
  $\operatorname{trop}_\infty D=\max(E\log2,\,O\log3)$.
  - $D$ is a binomial, so the only possible cancellation is between its two terms. That is a
    linear form in two logarithms, and Matveev bounds it (`polyLB_of_matveev`).
  - Away from the critical hyperplane $E\log2=O\log3$ no Baker input is needed (growth).

### 2.4 The tropical budget

**Proposition (derived here; $k\le2$ proved).** Let $c_1,\dots,c_n$ be partial cores of a family,
nonzero on the members considered. Suppose that for all $a\in\mathbb R^k_{\ge0}\setminus0$
$$\min_i\operatorname{trop}_\infty c_i(a)\ \lt\ \operatorname{trop}_\infty D(a).$$
Then, conditional on Matveev, only finitely many members off $\bigcup_iV(c_i)$ are cycles.

*Proof.*
1. On a cycle, $|D(\sigma_a)|\le\min_i|c_i(\sigma_a)|$.
2. Both sides are homogeneous of degree 1 in $a$. By compactness of the simplex, the strict
   inequality has a uniform margin $\eta\gt0$, so $\eta|a|\le O(\log|a|)$.
3. So $|a|$ is bounded. ∎

**With the rotation cores.** In this case $\operatorname{trop}c_i(a)=\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\log G_j+O(1)$,
where $G=\max(P,Q)$. The condition is then
$$\sum_ja_jg_j-\max_ia_ig_i\ \lt\ \max\Big(\sum_ja_jp_j,\ \sum_ja_jq_j\Big),\qquad g=\log G,\ p=\log P,\ q=\log Q.$$
Both sides are bilinear in $a$ against (weights $w$ on the cores, a mix $\theta$ of the two
pieces of $\operatorname{trop}D$). By von Neumann's minimax theorem the condition is equivalent to
the existence of fixed $(w,\theta)$ with every block's coefficient positive. That reduces to
$$\boxed{\ \min(A,B)\lt1\ },\qquad A=\sum_{p_j\gt q_j}\Big(1-\frac{q_j}{p_j}\Big),\quad B=\sum_{p_j\lt q_j}\Big(1-\frac{p_j}{q_j}\Big).$$

**Remarks.**
- Each term is below 1. In the gap alphabet, slopes are at least 1, so a below-block contributes
  at most $1-1/\log_23=0.369$. Hence **every independent-block family with at most two blocks of
  slope below $\log_23$ is inside the budget**, whatever its number of above-blocks.
- Same side ($A=0$ or $B=0$) never meets the critical hyperplane on the cone, so no Matveev is
  needed there. That is `TwoExp`, generalised.
- The loci $V(C(u_i,\mathrm{rest}_i))$ are where $x^\ast_F$ is constant along direction $i$. There
  $W$ is a cycle iff $u_i$ is (`twoExp_degenerate_u`). On the full torus they are zero sets of
  exponential polynomials, i.e. finite unions of cosets (Skolem–Mahler–Lech/Laurent). So the
  recursion goes to lower rank.
- **Checked.** `budget.py` computes $(A,B)$. An exact LP minimum of the margin agrees with
  $\min(A,B)\lt1$ on all 12 test families, including $(1)^3(2)^5$: $\min(A,B)=1.038$, LP margin
  $-0.0061$. Random sampling in 8 dimensions had missed that failing direction; the LP did not.

| blocks | $A$ | $B$ | budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1^a2^b$ | 0.208 | 0.369 | in (Straddle, proved) |
| $1^a2^b1^c$ | 0.208 | 0.738 | in |
| $1^a2^b1^c2^d$ | 0.415 | 0.738 | in |
| $1^a1^b1^c2^d$ (with spacers) | 0.208 | 1.107 | in, via $A$ |
| $(1)^3(2)^4$, interleaved | 0.830 | 1.107 | in |
| $(1)^3(2)^5$ | 1.038 | 1.107 | **out** |
| $(1)(3)(1)(3)(1)(3)$ | 1.415 | 1.107 | **out** |

The failing directions are interior and balanced ($E\log2\approx O\log3$, every block active).
That is the critical regime, where cancellation in $D$ and in the cores can conspire.

### 2.5 Beyond the budget: Vojta

- **Levin's theorem.** It is Vojta for the blow-up along $\mathcal V_F$, restricted to $S$-unit
  points. It gives $\log\gcd\le\varepsilon\,h(\sigma_a)$ outside finitely many translates of
  subtori.
- **In tropical terms.** That says *some* core has slope at most $\varepsilon$, ineffectively.
  Since $\operatorname{trop}D\asymp h$, it closes every family outside the exceptional subtori
  (`Vojta.cycles_of_levin`).
- **The effective programme between the two.** Look for partial cores better than the rotation
  supply on the failing cone:
  - Gröbner elimination onto quotient tori and onto sub-tori along cones;
  - Plücker combinations, as in `Nested.lean`.
  
  The failing region is a polyhedral cone, computable by the LP above, and the recursion runs on
  its faces.

Reading the table by column:
- **The orbit decides the method.** A ray ($k=1$) admits a full core. A higher-rank orbit admits
  only partial cores, and the budget decides whether they suffice. A non-orbit (towers) needs
  iteration.
- **The ∞ input is decided by the cone.** It is plain growth if the cone of exponents misses the
  critical hyperplane, Baker/Matveev if it crosses it, and the verification bound $X_0$ or Vojta
  if the budget fails there.
- **Two special shapes.** The **degenerate loci** are always "$x^\ast$ constant along a
  direction", so they are powers of a cycle. The **unit core** (Knight) is the extreme case, a
  ceiling of size 1 independent of length. It is the only exclusion here that is not about
  exponents growing.

## 4. What the scheme machinery is for

The maintainer's question was what schemes buy beyond elementary patterns. This review's answer
is **a decision procedure for which families are settled by which input**, in four steps:

1. **Compute the cores.** Present $W$ as a subgroup orbit in $\mathbb T_F$ and compute the
   elimination ideals of $\mathcal V_F$ onto the quotient tori. The rotation cores are a canonical
   supply; Gröbner elimination can give more.
2. **Tropicalise them**, at the valuation vector $(\log2,\log3)$.
3. **Solve the LP.** If the margin is positive, the family is settled (Baker/Matveev, effective,
   reduced by continued fractions as in `decide_twoexp.py`). If it is negative, the LP returns the
   failing cone.
4. **Recurse** on the faces of the failing cone and on the loci. What survives is the genuine
   Vojta/Levin regime, which is ineffective.

This systematises everything in §3 except the unit core. It also predicts new settled families
inside the budget but beyond $k=2$, e.g. $1^a2^b1^c2^d$. Not yet formalized.

## 5. Corrections to earlier documents

- **`Straddle.lean` docstring and `PATTERNS.md` §6.** They said the product of ceilings "is too
  weak for $k\ge3$" and that two exponents is the limit of the method. That is wrong. It compared
  *equal* weights against $\max\ge\sqrt{2^E3^O}$; the optimum over weights and over the two
  pieces of $\operatorname{trop}D$ is the budget of §2.4. Both texts are corrected.
- **RATIONALE §0.3 / §3.** "r ≥ 2: … the regime of Vojta's conjecture" should read: r ≥ 2 has no
  *full* core; it is the Vojta regime only where the partial cores fail the budget. A pointer is
  added there.

## 6. Next steps

- **Formalize the $k$-block budget theorem.** Generalise `twoExp_pow_le_of_polyLB`, which is the
  $k=2$ case:
  - $k$ rotation ceilings via `isCycleWord_rotate` and `wpow_append_ceiling`;
  - bounds $S(\mathrm{rest})\le\mathrm{len}\cdot\prod G_j^{a_j}$ and the analogue for $D$;
  - a rational certificate $(w,\theta)$ checked by `norm_num`.
- **The parity-alphabet version**, for $m$-cycles: the same identity in the free monoid on
  $\{0,1\}$ (`Dpv`/`g` in `CocycleCombine`). That would give finiteness of 2-cycles of either sign,
  conditional on Matveev.
- **Frontier families**, outside the budget: at least three below-blocks and $A\ge1$, e.g.
  $m$-cycles with $m\ge3$ or $(1)(3)(1)(3)(1)(3)$. Compute Gröbner partial cores on the failing
  cone and test whether they beat the rotation supply.
