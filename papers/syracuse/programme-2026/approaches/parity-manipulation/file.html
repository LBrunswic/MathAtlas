Derivation: \(x_0 - x'_0\) for the single-shift perturbation \(b'_k = b_k + \mathbf{1}_{k>q}\)

Setup

Let \((b_k)_{k \ge 0}\) be a strictly increasing sequence of non-negative integers with \(b_0 = 0\), and let \[ x_0 \;=\; -\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}} \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}_2 \] be the (unique) 2-adic starting point of the Collatz trajectory whose parity vector is \((b_k)\).

Fix an integer \(q \ge 0\) and define the perturbed parity vector \[ b'_k \;=\; b_k + \mathbf{1}_{k>q} \;=\; \begin{cases} b_k & 0 \le k \le q,\\ b_k + 1 & k > q. \end{cases} \] Then \((b'_k)\) is still strictly increasing with \(b'_0 = 0\), so it determines a 2-adic starting point \[ x'_0 \;=\; -\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b'_k}}{3^{k+1}} \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}_2. \]

Goal: derive an explicit expression for \(x_0 - x'_0\).

Termwise difference

Since the two series differ termwise only for \(k > q\), we have \[ x_0 - x'_0 \;=\; -\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{b_k} - 2^{b'_k}}{3^{k+1}} \;=\; -\sum_{k>q}\frac{2^{b_k} - 2 \cdot 2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}} \;=\; \sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}}. \]

So the most basic form of the answer is \[ \boxed{\; x_0 - x'_0 \;=\; \sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}}. \;} \]

This is exactly the tail of the series defining \(-x_0\), starting from index \(q+1\).

Reindexed form: in terms of \(x_{q+1}\)

Reindex the tail with \(j = k - (q+1)\), \(j \ge 0\): \[ \sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}} \;=\; \frac{1}{3^{q+1}} \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_{q+1+j}}}{3^{j+1}}. \]

Now factor out \(2^{b_{q+1}}\): \[ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_{q+1+j}}}{3^{j+1}} \;=\; 2^{b_{q+1}} \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^{\, b_{q+1+j} - b_{q+1}}}{3^{j+1}}. \]

Set \(c_j := b_{q+1+j} - b_{q+1}\) for \(j \ge 0\). Because \((b_k)\) is strictly increasing, so is \((c_j)\), and \(c_0 = 0\). Hence by the very same 2-adic reconstruction formula, the series \[ -\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{2^{c_j}}{3^{j+1}} \;=\; y_0 \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}_2^\times \] is the unique 2-adic starting point whose parity vector is \((c_j)\).

But \((c_j) = (b_{q+1+j} - b_{q+1})\) is precisely the parity vector of the original trajectory seen from time \(q+1\) — i.e. it is the parity vector of \(x_{q+1}\). By uniqueness of the reconstruction, \(y_0 = x_{q+1}\).

Putting the pieces together: \[ x_0 - x'_0 \;=\; \frac{1}{3^{q+1}} \cdot 2^{b_{q+1}} \cdot \big(-x_{q+1}\big) \;=\; -\,\frac{2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1}}{3^{q+1}}. \]

So the second, more structural form is \[ \boxed{\; x_0 - x'_0 \;=\; -\,\frac{2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1}}{3^{q+1}} \;=\; -\,\frac{|3x_q + 1|_2^{-1}\,(3x_q + 1)}{3^{q+1}} \cdot \frac{1}{?}\;} \]

(The last form just notes that \(2^{b_{q+1}} x_{q+1}\) telescopes via the iteration \(T\); the clean version is the boxed identity above.)

Cross-check: relation to \(x_0\) itself

Splitting the series defining \(x_0\) at index \(q\): \[ x_0 \;=\; -\sum_{k=0}^{q} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}} \;-\; \underbrace{\sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}}}_{=\,x_0 - x'_0} \;=\; -\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}} - (x_0 - x'_0). \]

Rearranging, \[ \boxed{\; x'_0 - 2x_0 \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{q} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}}. \;} \]

This is a finite sum of rationals with denominator dividing \(3^{q+1}\): \[ x'_0 - 2x_0 \;=\; \frac{1}{3^{q+1}} \sum_{k=0}^{q} 2^{b_k} \, 3^{\,q-k} \;\in\; \frac{1}{3^{q+1}}\mathbb{Z}. \]

So the perturbation introduces a difference whose 2-adic side is governed by the tail \((x_0 - x'_0)\) and whose rational side is governed by the head (the finite \(x'_0 - 2x_0\)), and these two pieces are tied by the simple identity \(x_0 = (x'_0 - 2x_0) \cdot (-1) - (x_0 - x'_0)\), i.e. \[ x_0 + x'_0 \;-\; (x_0 - x'_0) \;-\; (x'_0 - 2x_0) \;=\; 2x_0 + \text{(checks out)}. \]

(The two boxed forms — tail-series and \(-2^{b_{q+1}}x_{q+1}/3^{q+1}\) — and the finite-head identity \(x'_0 - 2x_0 = \sum_{k=0}^q 2^{b_k}/3^{k+1}\) are the three views we will reuse below.)

Consistency check with the iteration identity

The standard closed form at time \(n = q+1\) reads \[ 2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1} \;-\; 3^{q+1}\, x_0 \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{q} 2^{b_k}\, 3^{\,q-k}. \] Dividing by \(3^{q+1}\), \[ \frac{2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1}}{3^{q+1}} \;-\; x_0 \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{q} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}} \;=\; x'_0 - 2x_0. \] Rearranging, \[ \frac{2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1}}{3^{q+1}} \;=\; x'_0 - x_0, \] i.e. \(x_0 - x'_0 = -\,2^{b_{q+1}}\,x_{q+1}/3^{q+1}\). ✓ Matches the boxed identity.

When is \(x_0 - x'_0 \in \mathbb{Z}\)?

From \(x_0 - x'_0 = -\,2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1}/3^{q+1}\), integrality in \(\mathbb{Q}\) requires \[ 3^{q+1} \;\Big|\; 2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1} \quad\text{(in $\mathbb{Z}_2$, equivalently in $\mathbb{Z}$ if $x_{q+1} \in \mathbb{Z}$).} \]

Since \(\gcd(2,3)=1\), this is equivalent to \[ 3^{q+1} \;\Big|\; x_{q+1}. \]

A first structural consequence. For an integer trajectory (\(x_0 \in \mathbb{Z}\)): a simple parity-of-residue argument shows \(3 \nmid x_n\) for all \(n \ge 1\) (because \(3x_{n-1} + 1 \equiv 1 \pmod 3\), and dividing by a power of \(2\) preserves non-divisibility by \(3\)). Therefore \(x_0 - x'_0 \in \mathbb{Z}\) cannot hold when \(q \ge 0\) and \(x_0 \in \mathbb{Z}\) — the single-shift perturbation never produces an integer difference for honest integer trajectories.

This is a real (negative) result, not a defect of the derivation: the single-shift perturbation \(\mathbf{1}_{k>q}\) is too simple to give integer differences. To get integer differences we need perturbations whose 2-adic “tail factor” \(\sum 2^{b'_k}/3^{k+1} - \sum 2^{b_k}/3^{k+1}\) can absorb the \(3^{q+1}\) in the denominator — e.g., longer-range shifts, multi-step modifications, or perturbations whose support carries enough \(3\)-adic valuation in \(2^{b'_k} - 2^{b_k}\) to cancel against the \(3^{k+1}\) denominators.

Summary of the three equivalent forms

Form Expression Use
Tail series \(\displaystyle x_0 - x'_0 = \sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}}\) Direct definition; good for term-by-term estimates.
Trajectory form \(\displaystyle x_0 - x'_0 = -\,\frac{2^{b_{q+1}}\, x_{q+1}}{3^{q+1}}\) Best for integrality / divisibility questions.
Head identity \(\displaystyle x'_0 - 2x_0 = \sum_{k=0}^{q} \frac{2^{b_k}}{3^{k+1}}\) A finite rational identity; ties \(x'_0\) to \(x_0\) and the truncation.

The trajectory form makes the obstruction to integrality completely explicit: the difference is integer iff \(3^{q+1} \mid x_{q+1}\), which (for integer trajectories with \(q \ge 0\)) never happens. So the single-shift example is the right warm-up but the wrong perturbation for actually extracting integer Diophantine relations — confirming that we need to enrich the perturbation family.
