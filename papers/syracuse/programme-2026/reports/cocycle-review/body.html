Syracuse · cocycle program · review, limits and directions · {date}

The cocycle program is one gcd, gcd(S, D). Given Ellison and Barina's verification alone, no nontrivial positive cycle is a word of at most three runs over base blocks of total weight ≤ 9.5·108. Wedge-core certificates reach exactly three runs under Ellison; Rhin's bound extends them to any fixed number.

Every move in the program certifies that G = gcd(S(w), D(w)) is smaller than |D(w)|. Proved in Lean during this review:
  • Unconditional: the cycles of either sign with a balanced parity vector are exactly 1, −1 and −5 → −7 (Knight, Lothaire 2.1.15, averaging), for every gcd.
  • Given Ellison 1971: no cycle on any two-letter-run word 1adb, which includes Steiner's 1977 circuit theorem.
  • Given Ellison 1971: the only cycle (either sign) on two runs uavb of any standard Christoffel pair is −5 → −7, because the wedge core of every standard pair is 6-smooth, plus a kernel-checked enumeration for small E.
  • Given Ellison 1971, both signs: two letter runs, circuits (Steiner on ℤ: only 1, −1, −5, −7), level-(u,v) two-cycles and powers hold only the known cycles. Barina is no longer needed (SignedCycles.closed_classes). The −17 cycle lies outside every class.
  • Given Ellison 1971, both signs: two-block cycles (Simons 2-cycles) whose two big gaps are equal are only 1 and −5 → −7 (EqualTwoCycles.equal_two_blocks). Simons 2005 needed Baker and lattice reduction; the equal-gap half needs only Ellison. Distinct big gaps, the shape of −17, stay open.
  • Given Ellison 1971, beyond {{1,2}}: the Stern–Brocot tree grown from ([1],[d]) keeps the core 6-smooth × (2d−1 − 1). Its left spine (1, 1md) is closed for every d and exponent (NearPairs.near_pair_cycles). Every two-run cycle word over the tree has E < 20(d − 1), and the whole {{1,3}} tree is closed by a kernel-checked enumeration (DPairs.dpair_three_cycles). With Simons–de Weger 2005, no positive cycle lies on any of these trees, for every d (DTrees.pos_dpair_cycles). Negative cycles there need 3–7 equal big gaps, and a sound kernel check closes both signs for 3 ≤ d ≤ 7 (DTreeChecks.dpair_cycles_le_seven).
  • Given Ellison 1971, any blocks: over the tree grown from any non-commuting root (u₀, v₀), two-run cycle words have E < 20·log₂|C(u₀, v₀)| (Trees.no_cycle_tree). The two-run rung is effective block by block; for the −17 blocks (1112, 114), C = −3336 and E < 234.
  • Given Ellison 1971, three runs: a three-core identity makes D divide three explicit sums. For any three blocks with those sums nonzero, cycles have E < 20(K + 2) (ThreeRuns.three_runs_bounded); three letter runs 1adbec need E < 11(d + e + 2). With Ellison the method stops at four runs. The linear-programming suprema are 0.613, 0.804 and 0.890 for 2, 3 and 4 runs against Ellison's 0.856 (computed, not in Lean).
  • Given Rhin (Simons–de Weger Lemma 12), four runs: for any four blocks with the four sums nonzero, positive cycles have E < 3500 + 16K (FourRuns.four_runs_bounded). Each further fixed number of runs is one more finite check of this kind. The exact reach is cm = 1 − (log₂3 − 1)/((log₂3)m − 1) (§3), and with Hercher's cycle length all few-run words are excluded unless their blocks are astronomically large (RunsFrontier).
  • Given Ellison and Simons 2007 (negative 2-cycles, sketched): every negative cycle with at most two big gaps is −1, −5 → −7 or −17 (NegTwoCycles.only_known_of_few_blocks).
  • Repeat lemma (D7): a window repeated in a cycle word forces two cycle points 2E(u) apart. Given Ellison and Barina, every repeated window in a nontrivial positive cycle covers less than 51.37 % of E and 52.79 % of o (Repeat.repeat_half).
  • Unconditional, C-balance: two windows of equal length in a positive cycle differ in weight by at most 2·log₂(xmax/xmin) + (E − o·log₂3) (Estimators.c_balanced_cycle). PROGRAM.md §9.4 is resolved: S(w) is prime to 6, so the AM–GM estimator already sandwiches its 6-free part (Estimators.abelian_estimate).
  • Barina alone: every nontrivial positive cycle has o ≥ 72 057 431 991 odd steps (Eliahou's method with certified logarithms, CycleLength.cycle_length_ge), so the run frontier no longer needs Hercher's Corollary 29: ≤ 3 runs over base weight ≤ 9.5·108 with Ellison, ≤ 20 runs over ≤ 5.5·105 with Rhin (BarinaFrontier).
  • Two literature theorems now proved in Lean: Eliahou 1993 (cycles above 240 have E = 301994a + 17087915b + 85137581c, b ≥ 1, ac = 0; Eliahou.eliahou1993, with its 271 analogue eliahou_barina) and Terras 1976 (parity vectors ↔ residues mod 2k; Terras.terras). Both were previously assumed as stated propositions.
Open: words with four or more runs at every level. There the wedge-core size argument with Ellison provably falls short (linear-programming supremum 0.890 > 0.856); a Rhin/Baker bound, or Levin's ineffective theorem, is needed. Near-miss words show any lever there must determine G to within a factor k(w) of |D|.

1 · The program in one picture: rotations add nothing beyond (S, D)

On a cycle all rotation sums are multiples of D. For every word the recursion 2d₀S(1) = 3S + D, with D odd, shows that their gcd is already gcd(S, D). Every word is therefore a primitive cycle of x ↦ (3x + k)/2ν with k(w) = D/G, and the no-cycle conjecture says k(w) ≠ 1.

{pipeline}
Reading guide: left to right. The only quantity that matters is G. Every program move (frame strip, anchor split, wedge, combination round) is a certificate αS + βD that bounds G from above. Belaga and Mignotte (2006, Thm 4) state the 3x+1 conjecture in exactly this form: no member of the "Collatz corona" of C(ℓ−1, k−1) sums is divisible by 2ℓ − 3k.

The run-count hierarchy

Cut a word into runs with fixed letters or blocks and growing lengths. Then S and D become fixed polynomials in a few S-units, and the arithmetic of gcds of S-unit expressions applies rung by rung.

3 · Limits: the algebra is the same for every Syracuse-type map, so the excluding input must be arithmetic

Each identity of the program holds for every X, Y in every commutative ring. Changing 3x+1 to 3x+k changes only the output k(w); changing 3 to 5 changes Y. So a step built from those identities cannot by itself distinguish the maps below.

What any no-cycle proof must contain

  1. An exponential separation of 2E from 3o. No cycles implies |2a − 3k| ≥ (1+ε)k (Tao 2011, Prop. 7, sketched). So transcendence theory, or a new separation theorem, is unavoidable. The Baker/Rhin step of the squeeze is necessary, not decoration.
  2. An input specific to p = 3 or to the sign of D, at least for small o (negative cycles up to o = 7; 5x+1 up to o = 3).
  3. Resolution of G to within a factor k(w) on near-miss words. These are genuine 3x+k cycles with small k.
  4. Effectivity. In every published exclusion the only 3x+1-specific input is the verification bound N₀ (Hercher 2023). The Baker machinery itself is map-uniform: Steiner's method finds the 5x+1 cycles.

How far the wedge cores reach, exactly

For a word with m runs of fixed blocks, the cocycle gives m divisibilities D ∣ TR, one per excluded run. Each TR is a sum of m monomials 2(x-prefix)3(y-suffix) over the other runs (x = E, y = o·log₂3). Excluding a cycle needs a lower bound |D| ≥ 2θE with θ above the smallest of the m bounds. In the worst case that is cm = 1 − (log₂3 − 1)/((log₂3)m − 1): 0.613, 0.804, 0.890, 0.935, 0.961 for m = 2…6 proved in Lean for every m (Reach.reach_upper, reach_tight; Ellison lies strictly between c3 and c4: ellison_reach).

  • The lower bound. Take one run of slope (log₂3)m followed by m − 1 runs of slope 1 with weights in ratio log₂3, the whole word at the critical slope. Every excluded-run bound then telescopes to the same value, which gives cm.
  • Ellison (θ = 0.856): reaches m ≤ 3 (ThreeRuns).
  • Rhin (θ → 1 as E grows): reaches each fixed m, e.g. m = 4 (FourRuns). The required size grows like (log₂3)m.
  • The four-run failure under Ellison is real on actual words, e.g. 6a₀(1202)a₁(1212)a₂(1222)a₃ near the critical slope. All four bounds stay above 20.8557E up to E ≈ 55,000, with limiting ratio 0.878.
  • End to end, every m (MRuns.mruns_bounded): the m-block cocycle identity gives D ∣ TR, and with any separation |D| ≥ 2θ·max − L, cycles satisfy (θ − cm)·max(E, o·log₂3) ≤ K + L + log₂m. With Ellison this bounds every two- and three-run word of any blocks (mruns_ellison). The nonvanishing hypothesis is removed for long runs by a 2-adic argument. Given Ellison alone, any two or three runs of non-commuting blocks, each of weight above 6B + 1, satisfy E < 20(6B + 3.6) (MRunsExplicit.mruns_explicit).
  • No side conditions for ≤ 3 runs (MRunsDichotomy): T = 0 forces an orbit point onto a base block's own cycle. So, given Ellison, any cycle on ≤ 3 runs either has such a point or a bounded E. With Barina and Hercher: no nontrivial positive cycle is a word of at most three runs over base blocks of total weight ≤ 109 (frontier_le_three); with Barina alone, ≤ 9.5·108 (BarinaFrontier.frontier_le_three_barina).
  • Every number of runs, with Rhin (MRunsRhin): Rhin's bound is a separation with θ = 1 > cm for every m. So a nontrivial positive cycle on m runs either has an orbit point on a base block's cycle or has E ≤ σm(6B + 11 + 14⌈log₂o⌉ + log₂m). With Barina and Hercher: no nontrivial positive cycle on ≤ 10 runs over base weight ≤ 5·107, nor on ≤ 20 runs over base weight ≤ 6·105 (frontier_ten, frontier_twenty); with Barina alone, ≤ 10 runs over ≤ 5·107 and ≤ 20 runs over ≤ 5.5·105 (BarinaFrontier). The admissible weight shrinks like (log₂3)−m: 6,560 at 30 runs, 521 at 35, none from about 40.
  • Both signs (MRunsSigned): Rhin's bound is an absolute linear-form bound (as quoted by Rukhin, arXiv:1805.10496). It gives |2E − 3o| ≥ 2max − 2 − 14⌈log₂E⌉ for every word, so the all-m dichotomy holds for negative cycles too. That is the first effective statement for negative cycles with four or more runs.
  • PROGRAM.md's first objective, met for few-run words (ExplicitF.explicit_f): for any m-run word (no cycle assumed), some rotation gives a move representative D′S − A·D = 2E·T with |T| ≤ m·26B+1·2cm·max(E, o·log₂3). That is an explicit f(w) below 3o by 2−(1−cm)·o·log₂3 up to the block term, with sharp exponent.
  • Frontier (RunsFrontier, with Barina and Hercher's E > 1.375·1011): a nontrivial positive cycle that is a two-, three- or four-run word needs block coefficients above about 26·109.
  • Cycle length from Barina alone (CycleLength.cycle_length_ge): Eliahou's method, fully proved. Certified bounds on log 2 and log 3 place E/o strictly between the Farey neighbours 103768467013/65470613321 and 10439860591/6586818670, so every nontrivial positive cycle has o ≥ 72 057 431 991 and E ≥ 114 208 327 604. Every frontier above now has a Barina-only form (BarinaFrontier); Hercher's Corollary 29 is no longer needed, at the price of slightly smaller constants.

2-adic blindness. Its finite form is Terras's theorem, now proved in Lean (Terras.terras): each parity pattern of length k is realised by exactly one residue class mod 2k. Every (ax+b)/2 map with ab odd is conjugate to the 2-adic shift (Bernstein–Lagarias 1996; Akin 2004: "the method is too general"). Parity-pattern pigeonholes in ℤ₂ cannot see 3 against 5. Counting is map-uniform too heuristic: words of type (E,o) at the critical slope number about e(ln p − δ)o, with δ ≈ 0.056 nats per letter for p = 3 and 0.023 for p = 5 (o = 104). Counting cannot single out 3.

4 · Pigeonhole and simplification: the covering hypothesis is false as a word statement; what survives is arithmetic

  • Single moves need global near-periodicity. A move yields a bounded 6-free core only when two rotations agree outside one bounded window of size b. That forces gcd(shift, o) < b. Only O(o²·|A|2b) words qualify, against ≥ 2n/2 unbalanced words (Lipatov 1982; Heinis 2001). For b = 2 these are exactly the Christoffel classes (Pirillo 2000), whose core is a monomial. lit + derived
  • Multi-move chains reduce to gcd(S, D). Any ℤ-combination of rotations is αS + βD. A covering argument is therefore an S-unit gcd argument in disguise.
  • Zimin, Shirshov, Ramsey give tower or iterated-log length control and no χ-matching. Cut.
  • Additive (χ-matched) powers are forced but off the critical path. Over {{1,2,3,4}} every word of length 51 contains an additive square (Freedman–Brown 2016). Over finite subsets of ℤ in general, additive squares are open (Pirillo–Varricchio 1994; Fici–Puzynina 2023 survey); additive cubes are avoidable over {{0,1,3,4}} (Cassaigne–Currie–Schaeffer–Shallit 2014). But a local swap uu′ → u′u is not a difference of rotations, so G need not divide it.
  • The dichotomy that does work. A binary cyclic word is a Christoffel conjugate exactly when it is circularly balanced (Berstel–Lauve–Reutenauer–Saliola, Thm 6.9; Berstel–de Luca 1997). Christoffel parity words are excluded (Knight 2023), and balanced ⇒ Christoffel (Lothaire 2.1.15). Both are now in Lean, for every gcd and unconditionally: no nontrivial positive cycle has a balanced parity vector (BalancedPow.no_balanced_cycle_all). So every remaining cycle has an unbalanced parity word and an explicit unbalance witness: a canonical pair of rotations at which to anchor a move (D2).
  • Anchor geometry for an explicit f(w). In balanced words, abelian periods and anchor spacings are (semi)convergent denominators of the slope (Fici et al. 2016; Peltomäki 2020). C-balanced is equivalent to bounded abelian complexity (Richomme–Saari–Zamboni 2011). Cycle words are C-balanced proved: two windows of equal length ℓ ≤ o in a positive cycle satisfy E(u) − E(u′) ≤ 2·log₂(xmax/xmin) + (E − o·log₂3) (Estimators.c_balanced_cycle). The derivation's “+1” is the defect E − o·log₂3, which is below 1 only when 2E < 2·3o.
  • Repeat lemma proved. Transferred from Mahler's 3/2 problem (Dubickas 2009): a window repeated at two cyclic positions forces two cycle points 2E(u) apart (Repeat.repeat_bound). Given Ellison and Barina, a repeated window in a nontrivial positive cycle covers less than 51.37 % of E and 52.79 % of o (Repeat.repeat_half). It bites only on low-complexity words.

D4 · Continued-fraction renormalisation two runs, any level

Theorem
No positive cycle on words that have at most two runs of Christoffel blocks at some level of the continued fraction of log₂3.
Why
For blocks u, v, the TwoBackground algebra goes through unchanged, with the wedge core C(u,v) = D(u)S(v) − D(v)S(u) replacing 2d₁ − 2d₂: on a cycle D ∣ C(u,v)·D(ua) and D ∣ C(u,v)·D(vb) proved (TwoBackground.dvd_of_cycle_block). Sturmian words are recursively runs of two blocks.
Result
The feared growth of |C(u,v)| does not happen for standard pairs. C(u,uv) = X|u|C(u,v) and C(uv,v) = Ylen vC(u,v), so C is always −2i+13j (StdPairs.core_smooth). Then D ∣ D(ua), D ∣ D(vb), and Ellison closes it (StdPairs.no_cycle_std_pair). Still consistent with the near-misses, whose words use letters ≥ 3.
Beyond {{1,2}}
The core recursion holds from any root, so the tree grown from ([1],[d]) has core 6-smooth × (2d−1 − 1) (DPairs.core_dpair). Given Ellison, two-run cycle words over it have E < 20(d − 1). The {{1,3}} tree is closed by enumeration, and the left spine (1, 1md) for every d (NearPairs). Below that bound the word has at most 20 big gaps, so Simons–de Weger 2005 closes the positive side for every d (DTrees.pos_dpair_cycles). Negative cycles need 3–7 equal big gaps (neg_dpair_residual); a kernel check closes d ≤ 7 for both signs (DTreeChecks). From any root (u₀, v₀) with nonzero core the same argument gives E < 20·log₂|C(u₀, v₀)| (Trees.no_cycle_tree).
Three runs
The three-core identity gives D(W) ∣ TU, TV, TZ. For any blocks with these nonzero, Ellison bounds E < 20(K + 2) (ThreeRuns.three_runs_bounded). The same size argument fails from four runs on (linear-programming supremum 0.890 > 0.856).
Four runs
With Rhin's bound (SimonsDeWegerLemma12) in place of Ellison, four runs of any blocks are bounded for positive cycles: E < 3500 + 16K (FourRuns.four_runs_bounded).
Next
A uniform m-run statement needs a certificate that works for all m at once; the per-m checks grow like mm.
Stop if
The block ceiling exceeds |D| on the level-k near-misses.

Theorem

Why
It replaces 13.3 by about 4.1 in every squeeze and lowers every o₀.

D7 · Repeat lemma (Mahler 3/2) done

Theorem
If a cycle word has the same window u (length ℓ) at two positions, then 2o·2E(u)·|D| < 2E3o and 2o·3ℓ·|D| < 2E3o (Repeat.repeat_bound, either sign, unconditional).
Why
Equal windows give equal affine maps, so 2E(u)(xi+ℓ − xj+ℓ) = 3ℓ(xi − xj) (Dubickas's identity). Then 2E(u) ∣ xi − xj ≠ 0, and every cycle point satisfies 2o|x||D| < 2E3o. The derivation's xmax bound was replaced by this one; the 152·o14.3 form is not needed.
Result
Given Ellison: 10000·E(u) < 5850·o + 1443·E (repeat_linear). With the sandwich and Barina: a repeated window covers less than 51.37 % of E and 52.79 % of o (repeat_half). Runs and periodic stretches are repeated windows, so they are bounded too.
Limit
Vacuous for generic words, whose longest repeat is about 2·log₂o letters. It re-excludes Christoffel words, whose longest circular repeat is n − 2, and three-run words with one dominant run, but not three comparable runs derived, not in Lean.
