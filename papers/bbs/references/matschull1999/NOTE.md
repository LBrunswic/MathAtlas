# Matschull 1999: black hole creation in 2+1 dimensions

- **Source.** arXiv:gr-qc/9809087 v3 (11 Dec 1998); Class. Quantum Grav. 16 (1999) 1069.
- **File.** `pdf/gr-qc-9809087.pdf`, sha256 `11dafcbf…1b27d` (full hash in `../README.md`).
- **Pages.** References below are to PDF pages.
- **Checking.** Every quote was found with `pdftotext -q -f N -l N`, whitespace normalised.

## Verbatim quotes

1. PDF p.1, abstract: « When two point particles, coupled to three dimensional gravity with a negative
   cosmological constant, approach each other with a sufficiently large center of mass energy,
   then a BTZ black hole is created. »
2. PDF p.1, abstract: « Some general arguments imply that massive particles can be used as well, and
   the creation of a rotating black hole is also possible. »
3. PDF p.21: « The actual collision of the particles, and the creation of the future singularity,
   takes place behind the horizon at t = 0. »
4. PDF p.22: « Here, the Lorentz transformation represented by the holonomy u is not a rotation, but a
   boost, and µ is half of the hyperbolic angle that parametrizes this boost. »
5. PDF p.25: « The difference is that, if the energy lies above the threshold, then there are closed
   timelike curves in spacetime. »
6. PDF p.25: « For a vanishing cosmological constant, this spacetime is known as the Gott universe
   [8, 9]. »
7. PDF p.27: « The only difference is that the holonomy u has to be spacelike. »

## What it bears on in `../../NOTES-3+1.md` [r]

These are the planner's readings, not the paper's.

- **N5, Theorem G's physical reading,** « Matschull's creation of BTZ black holes from colliding
  particles in AdS ».
  - Quotes 1 and 3 are the creation result: two particles above threshold collapse to a BTZ black
    hole, the collision behind the horizon.
  - Quotes 4 and 7 say that the black hole's holonomy is a boost, not a rotation. This matches
    Theorem G's hyperbolic row: P_in hyperbolic, then a future BTZ-like singularity.
  - Quotes 5–6 tie the same threshold, with Λ = 0, to Gott's universe.
- **Scope.** Matschull's spacetimes are AdS₃ and explicit. BBS's theorem is local, at an interaction
  point. The correspondence is heuristic, as N5 says (« fits the table above »).
- **Massive particles.** Quote 2 says massive particles are covered by « general arguments » only.
  The explicit solution is for massless ones.
